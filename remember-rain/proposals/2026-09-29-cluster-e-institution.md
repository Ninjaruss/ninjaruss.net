## 1. The decision, stated as one question

Four items in the corpus are the same question wearing four vocabularies:

| # | Where | The question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Story Plan/Open Questions.md:7` | "Did the asylum predate Vesper? Is he its operator, public face, research subject, or something else?" |
| 2 | `Story Plan/Open Questions.md:11` | "What institution allowed a thirteen-to-eighteen-year-old Vesper to practice, and what did it believe it was authorizing?" |
| 3 | `remember-rain/decisions.md`, D-003 | "What this ruling does not decide: who operates the correction apparatus — Vesper alone, or the state with him as the engine." |
| 4 | `World/History.md:13` | "The Pursuer's long institutional career also predates the Flare." — asserted, employer never named |

And the bind that forces the issue. `World/History.md` names the city's
pre-Flare institutions, then rules both of them out of the function the Pursuer
performs:

- `:13` — "The island city, its dormant volcano, the water infrastructure, the
  Circuit Line, the Water Ministry, and Nimbus Co. all predate the supernatural.
  The Ministry administered water; Nimbus built civic infrastructure under
  contract. The Pursuer's long institutional career also predates the Flare."
- `:37` — "The Water Ministry's institutional power grows from water
  administration and resonance management, not from a separate supernatural
  government."
- `:39` — "Nimbus remains a contractor operating through Ministry contracts
  rather than a replacement state."

Meanwhile his work needs a body that owns custody, signatures, records, and
offers: `Characters/The Pursuer.md:3` "A man who has served the same institution
for thirty years"; `:53` "His signature appears on Aster's intake record.";
`Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:95` "the charges can be resolved";
`Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:35` "place Rain and Roxana in state custody."

**The question, stated once:** is the authority that licensed Vesper, that the
asylum answers to, and that operates the correction apparatus **one authority or
two** — and does the story ever let the reader see it?

**Two things already on the page that bear on the answer, and neither was written
to.** *Observation:* `World/History.md:31` lists what carries the five years —
"Water policy, corporate maintenance, **custody**, forms, and ordinary
infrastructure" — so custody is already inside the Ministry's historical
footprint. *Inference:* the Ministry's three form names
(`World/Locations.md:37`–`:41`, *Circulation Adjustment Notice*, *Resonance
Compliance Renewal*, *Household Flow Assessment*) presuppose field staff who
enter homes and serve notices, so a body of inspectors already exists by
implication in the Ministry's own stationery. The Pursuer needs an employer; the
corpus has already described one without naming it.

## 2. The trade, stated plainly

The decision is not a choice between a good answer and a bad one. It is a choice
between **thematic unity** and **Arc 2's comfort**, and the two pull against each
other:

- **Unity.** `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:181` — "Evil arrives through offers,
  forms, referrals, renovations, and schedules. It does not need to raise its
  voice." If Arc 2's shelter is licensed by the same authority whose loop thins
  districts in Arc 4, the horror is one system wearing several faces, which is
  what that line already claims the story does.
- **Comfort.** `Story Plan/Choice Points.md:35` — "Comfort matters. A pitfall that
  is merely boring or obviously wrong cannot tempt the player or respect the
  character choosing it." If the asylum reads as state infrastructure from the
  first scene, the Arc 2 pitfall is visibly a trap before it tempts anyone.

Every resolution below is a different settlement of that trade. **E1 buys unity
and pays in comfort. E3 buys comfort and pays in unity. E2 tries to hold both and
pays by never delivering the unity on the page.**

## 3. The three resolutions

### E1 — One authority, named in the plan layer as the Ministry's own expansion

**What it makes true.** The Ministry licensed Vesper, licenses the asylum he
runs, and built and operates the correction apparatus. No new body, no new name —
so no synonym is introduced and F-006's territory is untouched. It is the second
branch D-003 named ("the state with him as the engine"), resolved by taking the
one the document already describes at `World/History.md:21`: "The Water Ministry
expanded from managing circulation to managing the people and objects that
interacted with it."

**Why it satisfies the constraints rather than revising them.**

- `World/History.md:37` is *honoured*, not edited: this is not a separate
  supernatural government. It is the same government, five years further into an
  expansion `:21` already states.
- `World/Locations.md:45` ("Never give the Ministry a single villainous face") is
  satisfied because nothing is added but paper. The Ministry gains a licence, not
  a presence.
- `World/Magic System.md:183` ("Keep emotional or arc interpretation in Character
  and Story Plan references") is satisfied because the change lands in History,
  Locations, and Vesper's sheet — not in the magic document.

**What it gains beyond consistency.**

- `Characters/1 Vesper.md:59` — "He hands the group to the state as a referral,
  not a betrayal speech" — stops being a figure of speech. The referral goes to
  the authority that licenses him. The line gets better and needs no edit.
- `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:55` — "one obeys process; the other takes
  control because process is too slow" — becomes an intra-institutional contrast
  rather than a clash between unrelated forces. Two hands of one body.
- `Story Plan/Arc Structure.md:13` — "the expanding authority to determine what
  the person may become next" — now has an owner, so the "expanding authority" is
  the Ministry's rather than a character trait.
- `Story Plan/Open Questions.md:79` — "Which public account leaves Rain a
  fugitive after the correction apparatus is broken?" — gains a ready and
  unflattering answer: he destroyed civic infrastructure coupled to the city's
  water supply. The final fugitive status becomes causal rather than residual.

**What it costs — four, stated as costs and not as objections.**

1. **Arc 2's comfort.** The pitfall must tempt. This is the real price, and the
   only mitigation is execution: the Ministry appears at the asylum the way it
   appears everywhere, on paper. A licence, not a guard. This is a constraint the
   author must hold, not a fact the text can enforce.
2. **The finale becomes sabotage.** Rain breaks a Ministry installation coupled to
   the loop, and `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:123` and `World/Locations.md:25` both
   have the loop and apparatus physically collapsing. If the author does not want
   Rain to have broken the city's water, this argues against E1. Note the wording
   tension is already live and is F-012's to rule on: `World/Magic System.md:85`
   says "Breaking it stops the loop carrying the effect," while
   `World/Locations.md:25` says the loop breaks. E1 sharpens F-012 rather than
   resolving it.
3. **Vesper's culpability.** A licence is permission, not authority, and
   `Characters/1 Vesper.md:117` says "Do not portray five years of development as
   decades of authority." The draft text below names what the Ministry judged, not
   what Vesper earned. But if the author reads a licence as diluting him, that
   argues for E3.
4. **One residual stays open, deliberately.** Whether the Ministry *intended*
   city-scale correction, or whether Vesper exceeded the licence, is not settled
   by E1 — see the narrowed `Open Questions` entry below. E1 settles ownership;
   intent stays the author's.

**Status impact.** Closes the author-flagged question at `Open Questions.md:11`;
narrows `:7`; resolves D-003's residue toward the state branch; closes **W-c**;
bears on **F-016** (see knock-on list) without deciding it.

### E2 — One authority, settled in the plan layer and never shown on the page

**What it makes true.** Licensing, custody, and the apparatus are one authority
(the Ministry's expansion), and the story never lets the reader trace it. Arc 2's
shelter and Arc 4's clock are the same institution in the plan and unrelated in
experience.

**Text impact.** `World/History.md:13` and `:21` change as in E1 — the fact has
to be settled somewhere or `:13` stays a dangling assertion. `World/Locations.md`
`:63` and `:75` do **not** change, and `Characters/1 Vesper.md:19` does not
change. So E2 is cheaper than E1 by three edits and one document.

**What it costs.** It forfeits exactly what E1 gains. `Themes and Motifs.md:181`
says the horror arrives *through offers, forms, referrals, renovations, and
schedules* — things the reader encounters. A unity that lives only in `History.md`
is a consistency fix, not a delivery. It also leaves the strongest available
answer to `Open Questions.md:79` unwritten, since nothing on the page connects
Rain's act to a civic installation.

**When to prefer it:** if the author wants the consistency without changing what
Arc 2 feels like, and without committing to the finale-as-sabotage reading.

**Status impact.** Closes **W-c**; closes `Open Questions.md:11`; leaves `:7`
answered only in the plan layer, which is a status conflict the author should
clear deliberately rather than by omission.

---

### E3 — Two authorities, split deliberately

**What it makes true.** The asylum predates Vesper and is not the state's — a
private, charitable, or religious institution he came to run — while correction
is state-scale because it rides the Ministry's loop. The Pursuer's body is a
custody office the Ministry absorbed rather than founded.

**What it gains.**

- Arc 2's pitfall keeps its full comfort: the refuge genuinely is not the state's,
  so the trap is a person's certainty rather than an institution's reach.
- Vesper stays a private actor, which protects `Characters/1 Vesper.md:117` and
  keeps `:59`'s "not a betrayal speech" figurative.
- It honours `Open Questions.md:7`'s first clause — "Did the asylum predate
  Vesper?" — with a yes, which is a real answer rather than a deferral.

**What it costs.**

- It needs a sentence in a document that owns *who may operate infrastructure*,
  and no document does. `World/History.md` owns institutions, `World/Locations.md`
  owns places, `World/Magic System.md:183` excludes institutions. This is the same
  structural gap identified in Cluster A of the companion document: some
  propositions have no legal home.
- It gives up the unity. Arc 2's shelter and Arc 4's clock stay unrelated, so
  `Themes and Motifs.md:181` keeps describing a method the back half does not
  demonstrate.
- It raises a question E1 does not have to answer: why does a state operating a
  city-scale correction apparatus tolerate an independent extractor doing
  contact-range work? `Characters/1 Vesper.md:79`–`:99` gives him an archive and a
  practice with no licensing anywhere. Under E3 that needs a reason, or Vesper's
  independence reads as an oversight.

**When to prefer it:** if preserving Arc 2's comfort and Vesper's privacy is worth
more than the horror being one system.

**Status impact.** Closes **W-c** with a different answer than E1; closes
`Open Questions.md:7` and `:11`; leaves D-003's residue resolved toward
"the state is not the engine, and the apparatus's operator is unnamed."

## 4. Recommended: E1

Recommended on one ground that the other two do not have. E1 is the only
resolution where the story's own declared method of delivering evil
(`Themes and Motifs.md:181`) and its own ending requirement
(`Open Questions.md:79`, `0 What is Remember Rain.md:33`) are both satisfied by
the same fact. E2 settles the fact and forfeits the delivery; E3 keeps the
delivery and gives up the fact.

Its cost is real and it is named above: Arc 2's comfort is bought with restraint
in execution rather than with a text change. That is a cost the author holds for
the length of Arc 2 — and it is the kind of cost this story already asks its
author to hold elsewhere (nobody points at the Widower; no character names the
hand motif; the steam's reaction to Rain's question is never identified:
`Characters/The Pursuer.md:109`).

## 5. Scrivener-ready replacement text — E1

Five passages, three documents. Each is quoted as it currently reads so it can be
found by text; each replacement keeps the host document's voice, heading depth,
and list style, and is the smallest change that resolves W-c.

### 5.1 `World/History.md:13`

**Replace this passage:**

> The island city, its dormant volcano, the water infrastructure, the Circuit
> Line, the Water Ministry, and Nimbus Co. all predate the supernatural. The
> Ministry administered water; Nimbus built civic infrastructure under contract.
> The Pursuer's long institutional career also predates the Flare.

**With:**

```md
The island city, its dormant volcano, the water infrastructure, the Circuit Line, the Water Ministry, and Nimbus Co. all predate the supernatural. The Ministry administered water, and employed the assessors, inspectors, and custody staff that administering it required; Nimbus built civic infrastructure under contract. The Pursuer's long institutional career also predates the Flare, and it was spent in the Ministry's service.
```

**Why this wording.** It names the employer using staff categories the Ministry's
own form names already presuppose (`World/Locations.md:37`–`:41`) and that
`World/History.md:31` already lists under "custody." It introduces no name and
therefore no synonym. "Assessment" is used deliberately, because
*Household Flow Assessment* is already the Ministry's word for the visit.

### 5.2 `World/History.md:21`

**Replace this passage:**

> The city discovered that its largely closed volcanic/Sacred Water circulation
> retained an unusually strong reservoir. Periodic high-resonance conditions
> continued to permit new passives and Imprints long after most Flare resonance
> elsewhere had dissipated. The Water Ministry expanded from managing circulation
> to managing the people and objects that interacted with it. *Deviation* became
> an administrative category. Nimbus built or adapted the Imprint Vault and
> sensory-deprivation pods under Ministry contracts.

**With:**

```md
The city discovered that its largely closed volcanic/Sacred Water circulation retained an unusually strong reservoir. Periodic high-resonance conditions continued to permit new passives and Imprints long after most Flare resonance elsewhere had dissipated. The Water Ministry expanded from managing circulation to managing the people and objects that interacted with it. *Deviation* became an administrative category. The Ministry licensed treatment sites for passives it judged manageable, and coupled the correction apparatus to the loop at the caldera. Nimbus built or adapted the Imprint Vault and sensory-deprivation pods under Ministry contracts.
```

**Why this wording.** One inserted sentence, placed before the Nimbus sentence so
the paragraph still closes on the contractor, as it does now. "Treatment sites"
is plural and unspecified, which keeps `Open Questions.md:7` genuinely open about
the asylum's origin while settling who licensed it.

### 5.3 `World/Locations.md:61`–`:63` — the asylum entry

**Leave the heading unchanged** — F-016 owns the name "Abandoned Asylum," and this
change must not decide it.

**Replace this passage (the body under the heading):**

> Vesper's sanctuary and treatment space: pleasant, open-doored, and
> task-oriented. It should feel like a place that offers care and enclosure at the
> same time.

**With:**

```md
A Ministry-licensed treatment site and Vesper's sanctuary: pleasant, open-doored, and task-oriented. It should feel like a place that offers care and enclosure at the same time. The licence shows on paper and never on a uniform.
```

**Why this wording.** "The licence shows on paper and never on a uniform" is the
execution constraint from §3, written as a drafting rule — the same shape as the
surrounding rules in this document (`:21` "Avoid overt dystopian spectacle.";
`:45` "Never give the Ministry a single villainous face."). It is what keeps Arc
2's pitfall tempting, and it is the one line in this package that E1 cannot afford
to drop.

### 5.4 `World/Locations.md:73`–`:75` — the caldera entry

**Replace this passage (the body under the heading):**

> The final ascent and the hub at the center of the volcanic system. Together they
> provide the last gate, Vesper's chamber, and the physical breaking point of the
> water loop.

**With:**

```md
The final ascent and the hub at the center of the volcanic system. Together they provide the last gate, Vesper's chamber, and the physical breaking point of the water loop. The apparatus is Ministry infrastructure, and the group reaches it without authorisation.
```

**Why this wording.** It states the institutional fact as a fact about the place
and its access, which is what this document owns. It does not restate who breaks
it or why — that is `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md`'s, and `:121` already carries it.

### 5.5 `Characters/1 Vesper.md:19`

**Append one sentence to the existing paragraph. Replace this passage:**

> He was thirteen when the Flare happened. The resonant alteration gave him
> unusually complete encoding and retrieval: perfect recall and involuntary replay
> are parts of the same memory architecture. Five years of experimentation and
> escalating success have made his conclusions feel complete because he reached
> them quickly and has forgotten none of the evidence.

**With:**

```md
He was thirteen when the Flare happened. The resonant alteration gave him unusually complete encoding and retrieval: perfect recall and involuntary replay are parts of the same memory architecture. Five years of experimentation and escalating success have made his conclusions feel complete because he reached them quickly and has forgotten none of the evidence. The Ministry licensed him to treat at fourteen, having judged that relief arrived faster than supervision could.
```

**Why this wording.** It answers `Open Questions.md:11`'s second half — "what did
it believe it was authorizing?" — with the Ministry's reasoning rather than
Vesper's merit, which is what keeps `:117`'s "Do not portray five years of
development as decades of authority" intact. A licence is permission; the
sentence is careful to give him permission and nothing else.

**Two alternatives for the author's pick, since the age is not derivable from any
document:**

- **Plainer reasoning:** "The Ministry licensed him to treat at fourteen. It
  judged that the results justified the arrangement."
- **No age at all:** "The Ministry licensed him to treat while he was still a
  child, having judged that relief arrived faster than supervision could." — this
  avoids fixing a number that no other document needs, and leaves
  `Open Questions.md`'s "thirteen-to-eighteen" span as the only dating in canon.

### 5.6 `Characters/The Pursuer.md:17` — recommended, lighter touch

**Replace this passage:**

> His career predates the Flare; his supernatural condition does not. Thirty years
> of service supplied the heading, and the last five years turned endurance and
> wanting into fuel.

**With:**

```md
His career predates the Flare and was spent in the Ministry's service; his supernatural condition does not. Thirty years of service supplied the heading, and the last five years turned endurance and wanting into fuel.
```

**Why here and not `:3`.** `:3` is the sheet's opening line — "A man who has
served the same institution for thirty years and calculated when the Furnace will
run empty." Naming the Ministry there would spend the facelessness
`World/Locations.md:45` protects on the sheet's most quoted sentence. Naming it in
`:17`'s prose accomplishes the same fix at lower visibility. **The alternative is
to change neither**, on the grounds that `World/History.md:13` now carries the
identification and a character sheet is allowed to say "the same institution."

### 5.7 `Story Plan/Open Questions.md:7` and `:11`

`:7` and `:11` cannot stay as they are. E1 answers them, and
`Open Questions.md:3` is explicit: "Only questions without a usable current answer
belong here. Do not treat earlier speculative wording as canon." Leaving an
answered question in the register is a status conflict of exactly the kind this
space exists to catch.

**`Open Questions.md:7` — replace this line:**

> - Did the asylum predate Vesper? Is he its operator, public face, research subject, or something else?

**With:**

```md
- Did the asylum building predate Vesper?
```

E1 answers "is he its operator." Whether the building came first does not, and it
is a live question the answer to E1 leaves untouched.

**`Open Questions.md:11` — replace this line:**

> - What institution allowed a thirteen-to-eighteen-year-old Vesper to practice, and what did it believe it was authorizing?

**With:**

```md
- Did the Ministry authorise correction at city scale, or did Vesper exceed the licence it gave him?
```

This is the residual E1 deliberately leaves open, and it is a sharper question
than the one it replaces: it is the question the author actually has to answer to
write Arcs 4 and 5, and it is the one `Characters/1 Vesper.md:45` already
describes in miniature — "he treats successful present intervention and present
consent as sufficient authority to close another person's future revision." It
keeps the entry's function rather than deleting it.
