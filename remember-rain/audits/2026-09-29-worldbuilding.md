### W-a — "Wanting" is the object of three documented operations and is defined in no World document

**Type:** underdefinition
**Severity:** significant
**Evidence:** `World/Magic System.md:81` — "His hands can isolate one
memory-network; the loop cannot, so what a corrected district loses is wanting in
general — the appetite for a thing rather than the record of it."
**Also:** `World/Magic System.md:87` — "Exposure degrades wanting in proportion
to time and concentration."
**Also:** `World/Magic System.md:65` — "The Pursuer burns the wanting that once
drove him."
**Also:** `World/Magic System.md:149` — "Vesper cannot extract a want as a
separate object. He removes the memories and associations that sustain it."
**Also:** `World/Magic System.md:91` — "Correction removes wanting, not memory. A
corrected person can still say what happened and cannot say what they want."
**Also:** `Story Plan/Arc Structure.md:13` — "Can you keep wanting when wanting
hurts?"
**Also:** `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:47` — "He offers relief from the ache of
wanting."
**Also:** `Story Plan/Choice Points.md:43` — pitfall 2 protects "the present from
painful wanting".
**Also:** `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:169` — "`unlit` — wanting removed"

**Observation:** Three documented mechanics act on "wanting," with three
different verbs, and no World document defines it. Correction thins it and
degrades it in proportion to exposure; Furnace burns the charge attached to it;
extraction removes the memories and associations that sustain it. The term also
appears in the arc questions, in two pitfall descriptions, and as the referent of
one of the six gold-vocabulary words. The 2026-09-16 terminology index
(`audits/2026-09-16-terminology-index.md`) contains **no row for "wanting"** — it
was never inventoried, so its absence of definition has never been recorded.

**Inference:** the three operations are precisely specified and their shared
object is not. A reader can say exactly what Vesper does and exactly what the
loop does, and has no word for the thing being done to the person. Because
"wanting" is also the word the arc questions turn on and the word `unlit` glosses,
the undefined term is load-bearing in four registers at once.

**Why it matters:** Arc 2's pitfall, Arc 4's reveal, and the Pursuer's cost all
describe a loss using a term the world does not own. Arc 2's whole argument is
that relief can genuinely help and can also replace participation
(`Story Plan/Arc Structure.md:13`) — which requires knowing what was relieved.
Distinguishing which of the three operations is happening to a given character is
currently the only way to tell "help" from "subtraction," and the object that
distinction is drawn on is undefined.

**Counter-argument, recorded because it may be decisive:**
`World/Magic System.md:25` states that the world withholds public terms
deliberately — "There is no public technical term inside the story, so names such
as the Ghost and Static are personal." An unglossed "wanting" may be that same
choice made a second time. A definition that removes a deliberate absence would
be a regression, and this finding does not assume otherwise.

**Related but not part of this finding:** Pull is already defined
(`World/Magic System.md:27`, "the interaction creates an unmistakable
attraction"), and the newest prose deliberately plays Pull and wanting against
each other — `Manuscript/Arc 1 - Fugitive/2 The vault.md:77` "it was clear
something was pulling my mind here" and `:175` "But I \*want\* the goggles." Two
attraction-words sharing a metaphor is not drift there; it is the scene working.
Any definition must therefore define wanting *against* Pull rather than as a
synonym for it.

**Open question for the author:** is "wanting" unglossed on purpose — the world's
own version of a condition with no public term — or is it the missing axis the
three mechanics already imply?

**Candidate resolutions:**

1. **Define wanting as a world term, explicitly distinguished from Pull.** State
   in `### Costs` or a short adjacent subsection that wanting is a person's
   appetite for a specific thing while Pull is resonance-compatibility attraction
   toward an Imprint, then give correction, extraction, and Furnace one line each
   against that object. *Cost:* touches the closed vocabulary —
   `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:161`'s `faded` and `:169`'s `unlit` both gloss
   wanting and would need re-reading — and it decides the counter-argument above.
2. **Define the relation without defining the object.** State only how the three
   operations differ in layer — appetite thinned, sustaining memory removed,
   accumulated charge spent — and leave "wanting" itself unglossed. *Cost:*
   partial legibility: the three mechanics become comparable without the world
   gaining a word for what the sufferer lost. Preserves the deliberate absence.
3. **Leave it.** *Cost:* the three mechanics keep acting on an undocumented thing,
   and each of the four registers that names it keeps relying on a term the world
   does not own. No scene is currently blocked by this.

**Status:** open

### W-c — `World/History.md` asserts a pre-Flare institution for the Pursuer while constraining both named bodies so that neither can hold it

**Type:** causal gap
**Severity:** significant
**Evidence:** `World/History.md:13` — "The island city, its dormant volcano, the
water infrastructure, the Circuit Line, the Water Ministry, and Nimbus Co. all
predate the supernatural. The Ministry administered water; Nimbus built civic
infrastructure under contract. **The Pursuer's long institutional career also
predates the Flare.**"
**Also:** `World/History.md:37` — "The Water Ministry's institutional power grows
from water administration and resonance management, not from a separate
supernatural government."
**Also:** `World/History.md:39` — "Nimbus remains a contractor operating through
Ministry contracts rather than a replacement state."
**Also:** `Characters/The Pursuer.md:3` — "A man who has served the same
institution for thirty years and calculated when the Furnace will run empty."
**Also:** `Characters/The Pursuer.md:17` — "Thirty years of service supplied the
heading, and the last five years turned endurance and wanting into fuel."
**Also:** `Characters/The Pursuer.md:53` — "His signature appears on Aster's
intake record."
**Also:** `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:95` — "The Pursuer presents a genuine
exchange: surrender the listed property, including the goggles, and the charges
can be resolved."
**Also:** `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:35` — "Rain's \*not yet\* keeps the route
moving until Roxana's Melt and Claire's referral place Rain and Roxana in state
custody." (`Story Plan/Arc Structure.md:13` — "Rain and Roxana are referred to
the state.")
**Also:** `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:55` — "**Pursuer / Vesper:** one obeys
process; the other takes control because process is too slow."
**Also:** `World/History.md:23` — "Known extractions, Melts, investigations, and
institutional cases fall within these five years."

**Observation:** `World/History.md` lists the city's pre-Flare institutions and
then asserts one more — the Pursuer's — without naming it. His work requires a
body that owns custody, signatures, records, and offers: he signs an intake
record, presents an amnesty whose charges can be "resolved," completes a
transfer, and delivers people into state custody. Both of the bodies `History`
does name are constrained away from that function at `:37` and `:39` — the
Ministry's power grows from water administration and not from a separate
supernatural government, and Nimbus remains a contractor rather than a
replacement state.

**Inference:** the enforcement-and-custody function has neither a name nor a
document. That is distinct from F-006, which concerns the names the state
carries for itself, and distinct from D-003's residue, which concerns only the
correction apparatus. Three questions in the corpus turn out to be the same
question — who licensed Vesper (`Story Plan/Open Questions.md:11`), who owns the
asylum (`:7`), and who operates the correction apparatus (D-003) — and the
Pursuer's employer is a fourth face of it.

**Why it matters:** Arc 2's argument is stated as "the expanding authority to
determine what the person may become next" (`Story Plan/Arc Structure.md:13`).
An expanding *authority* needs an owner: without one, the referral at Arc 2
scene 10 and the amnesty at Arc 4 scene 7 are acts by a person rather than by an
institution, and the arc's horror is a character trait rather than a system.
`Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:55` makes process one half of the Pursuer's
contrast with Vesper, and a process with no owner cannot be contrasted with
anything. It also means Arc 2's pleasant, open-doored shelter and Arc 4's
advancing boundary are currently unrelated institutions even though
`Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:181` requires the horror to arrive "through
offers, forms, referrals, renovations, and schedules" — that is, through one
system wearing several faces.

**Calibration, and why this is not severity `blocking`:** no scene is blocked by
it. The Pursuer can present papers the player does not need to trace, and Arc 2's
scenes can be staged as written. What is blocked is settling the *ownership* of
the arc's central pressure. `significant` is the honest severity; calling it
blocking would inflate the register.

**Open question for the author:** is the asylum's origin the same fact as the
apparatus's operator, or two facts to be kept apart? `Story Plan/Open
Questions.md:7`, `:11`, D-003's residue, and `World/History.md:13`'s unnamed
institution all key on this one answer.

**Candidate resolutions:**

1. **One authority at three altitudes, held by the Ministry rather than by a new
   body.** The authority that licensed Vesper, owns or licenses the asylum, and
   operates the apparatus is the Ministry's own expansion — which `History.md:21`
   already describes ("The Water Ministry expanded from managing circulation to
   managing the people and objects that interacted with it"). This honours
   `:37`'s constraint instead of revising it, invents no name and therefore
   creates no synonym (F-006's territory), and keeps the Ministry faceless per
   `World/Locations.md:45`. *Cost:* Arc 2's comfort depends on the asylum not
   reading as state infrastructure, so the Ministry's presence there must stay at
   the altitude of forms and never of uniforms; and Vesper's file needs a line
   about authorization that does not become institutional authority, per
   `Characters/1 Vesper.md:117` ("Do not portray five years of development as
   decades of authority").
2. **Vesper alone; the Pursuer's employer remains unnamed.** *Cost:*
   `World/History.md:13` continues to assert an institution no document
   describes; Vesper remains unlicensed at thirteen, which
   `Story Plan/Open Questions.md:11` is explicitly uneasy about; and
   `Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:55` gives half its contrast to an ownerless
   process.
3. **Split it deliberately: the asylum predates Vesper and is not the state's,
   while correction is state-scale because it rides the Ministry's loop.**
   *Cost:* a coherent position — "the state with him as the engine but not as its
   author" — but it needs a sentence in a document that owns who may operate
   infrastructure, and no document currently does.

**Status:** open

## Indexed and deliberately not raised

Both were surfaced by this pass and are recorded so they are not re-derived. Each
has a real reason for not being a finding, stated rather than implied.

**W-b — "district" is Arc 4's unit of loss and is defined nowhere; so is its
reporting unit, "anchor station."** `World/Magic System.md:79`, `:89`,
`World/Locations.md:21`, `Story Plan/1 The Spine.md:85`, `:89`, and
`Story Plan/Themes and Motifs.md:61` all run Arc 4's clock on districts, and
`World/History.md:27` measures the advance in "an anchor station" — which the
2026-09-16 index already records as "undefined — never defined as a Circuit Line
term." **Not raised because** a ruling closes it immediately: the options are
whether districts are water-supply zones or administrative units, and either
answer defines both terms at once. Opening a register row that the next ruling
deletes is bookkeeping, not a finding. It belongs in the decision queue, where it
is: `proposals/2026-09-29-worldbuilding-integration.md`, Cluster C.

**W-d — the water-maintenance layer and the surveillance layer are established in
the newest manuscript scene and in no World document.**
`Manuscript/Arc 1 - Fugitive/2 The vault.md:35` (worker shed, yellow rain
jackets, whiteboard of chores), `:41` (the "Daily Boiler and Circulation Log"),
`:73` (confiscated household objects filed by ID), `:159` ("SECURITY RECORDINGS
WILL BE REVIEWED."), `:207` ("A camera stared down at me above the doorway."),
`:219` (maintenance tunnels) are all post-plan prose, and
`Characters/Shiori.md:81` scales a confiscated recorder into "unattended
surveillance infrastructure" that the index already notes as
"`World/Locations.md` (not covered)." **Not raised because** nothing is broken:
the prose carries both layers, so there is no gap a scene could fall into — only
a consistency risk if later scenes re-invent vocabulary this scene already fixed.
It becomes the change itself, not a finding, if the author accepts Cluster D1 or
H1 of the companion document.
