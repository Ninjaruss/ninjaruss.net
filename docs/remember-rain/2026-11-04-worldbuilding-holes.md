# Remember Rain — worldbuilding holes

**Date:** 2026-11-04
**Sources:** `World/History`, `World/Locations`, `World/Magic System`,
`Story Plan/Arc Structure`, `Story Plan/0 What is Remember Rain`,
`Story Plan/1 The Spine`, all 50 card briefs, the character sheets.
**Test used:** a gap is only a hole if a scene you have to write needs it.
Everything below is ranked by what it blocks, not by how incomplete it looks.

---

## First: the world is not the weak part

The honest headline is that `World/History` and `World/Locations` are better than
the usual state of this kind of document. The Flare is **one event, five years
back, non-recurring** — a single hard constraint that kills an entire category of
escalation problem. Correction advancing through *accepted relief* rather than
force is a real idea, not set dressing. And the Ministry's register is specified
at the level of paperwork — *Circulation Adjustment Notice*, *Resonance Compliance
Renewal*, *Household Flow Assessment* — which is more usable than three paragraphs
of atmosphere would be.

What is actually missing is narrower and more fixable than "the worldbuilding is
thin." It is four specific things, and two of them block scenes in Arc 1.

---

## 1. The goggles have no stated function — and your own prose knows what it is

**Blocks: Arc 1 cards 2 and 3, Arc 4 card 7, Arc 5 card 7. The inciting object.**

The goggles are mentioned **36 times** across the package. `World/Magic System`
says only that the bond survives extraction and that Rain can "understand and
deliberately use them." Nothing anywhere says **what they do.**

This is the single largest hole in the project, because the object is the reason
the plot happens, it is on the Arc 4 amnesty manifest, and Arc 5 card 7 turns on
Rain using it under pressure.

And there is a nearly-written answer sitting in the package. Card 2's brief says
the **"worker's-refusal Imprint"** plays when the goggles call to him. That phrase
appears exactly twice, both in that one card, and is defined nowhere. Meanwhile
your drafted vault prose has built the room around it:

> *"It seems to be a storage spot for workers to drop off their equipment … Thick,
> yellow rain jackets hang on a nearby rack. A large whiteboard, with poorly erased
> smudges and pinned papers … Mostly chores, procedures, the usual list of things
> someone decided workers needed reminding about. Either these sheets were replaced
> recently, or nobody actually reads them."*

A confiscated Imprint of a worker's refusal, in a room full of abandoned chore
lists, calling to a man who cannot start his own work. That is the book's thesis
as an object, and it is three sentences from being canon.

**What to decide:** what the goggles *do* when used, and whether the worker's
refusal is the Imprint's content, its origin, or both. `{TK}`

---

## 2. Rain has nowhere to live, and the drift is set there

**Blocks: Arc 1 card 4 — the card you are drafting tonight.**

The word **"safehouse" appears once in the entire package**: in card 4's
cast-and-place line. It is not in `World/Locations`, which lists eleven other
places including a freight line and an alley.

Card 3 is "his apartment." Card 4 is "the safehouse." Card 7 is "the hideout,"
which belongs to Claire and Roxana — and he does not meet them until card 6. So
card 4 is a room he found alone, between those two scenes, and nothing describes
it, says whose it is, or says how he is paying for it.

The drift needs a ceiling, a fridge and a routine. Two of those are property.

**The sharper version of this question**, and the reason it is interesting rather
than just missing: in a city where the Ministry administers *water*, with a form
called *Household Flow Assessment*, **how does an unregistered person drink?** A
fugitive in this city is not someone hiding from police. He is someone who cannot
be metered. That is the correct register for this book — evil arriving as a form —
and the world documents set it up without ever firing it.

**What to decide:** where he is, whose it is, and whether water is how the city
notices people. `{TK}`

---

## 3. The disappearances are never explained, and may contradict the asylum

**Blocks: Arc 1 cards 3 and 6, Arc 2 card 1.**

"Disappearance" and "missing" appear in **zero** world documents and **zero**
times in `Arc Structure`. They exist only in card briefs and the Spine. Nothing
states who disappeared, how many, how the public found out, or why the Ministry
has a theory.

Worse, there is a possible logic collision. The premise says Rain is "blamed for
disappearances caused by someone who can remove memory and wanting." But Arc 2
card 1 says the asylum is **"calm, useful, and voluntary"** and that Rain
recognises the missing people there — and Arc 2's whole argument depends on
Vesper's relief being genuinely chosen. If the residents walked in voluntarily,
there are no disappearances. There are adults who stopped answering their phones.

That is a much weaker thing to pin on a man, and it would not generate "official
numbers and a plausible theory."

**Three ways out, and they are genuinely different stories:**

- **The Ministry needs a cause.** Attrition is real and voluntary, and a named
  thief is cheaper than admitting people are walking out of their lives. Fits
  "evil arrives through offers and forms" exactly, and makes Rain's charge a
  *clerical convenience* — which is the most frightening option and the closest
  to your seed's "they never considered this was best for me."
- **Vesper takes some and invites others.** The early ones were taken; by the
  story's present he has learned that asking works better. Preserves the crime
  and dates the horror.
- **Families report it as disappearance because the person who came back is
  wrong.** Nobody is missing. The Widower's argument becomes the public one.

**What to decide:** which. `{TK}` This one changes Arc 1 and Arc 2 together.

## 4. The authority has three names and one of them is undefined

**Blocks: nothing yet, but it will surface in every Arc 1–2 line of dialogue.**

- `World/Locations` and `World/History`: **the Water Ministry** (5 uses), **Nimbus
  Co.** (19).
- Card briefs: **"the System"** — and it appears in no world document at all.

Five card briefs used a bare "System": cards 2 and 5 of Arc 1, card 10 of Arc 2,
and Arc 3's Intake. (Two of those were my own wording in the card 2 brief, carried
over from the old one; I have corrected that to Nimbus, and card 5 is cut.)

This may not be an error. "The System" is plausibly what a citizen *calls* them,
and the Ministry's own register is deliberately bland — it never says *deviant* or
*correction*. A bitter shorthand in Rain's narration against sedated civic
language on the forms is a good effect.

But it is undocumented, so right now it reads as inconsistency rather than voice,
and you will have to re-decide it every time Rain refers to them.

**What to decide:** one line in `World/Locations` saying who calls them what.
Cheap, and it settles a lot of future sentences. `{TK}`

## 5. The locked final line exists in two versions

**Not worldbuilding, but found on the way and too important to leave.**

| Where | Line |
|---|---|
| `Arc 1 · 0 Rain intro` brief, marked `[ending callback]` | "In spite of it all, **I am** Rain." |
| `0 What is Remember Rain`, under "The locked ending" | "In spite of it all, **I am** Rain." |
| `Arc 5 · 10 The outbound train` brief | "In spite of it all, **I choose to be** Rain." |

And the Spine's card 10 instructs: *"End with the exact selected callback from
`Arc 1 - Fugitive/0 Rain intro.md`."* So the Spine demands exactness while that
card's own brief states a different wording.

Both serve the premise's note that this is "a present claim, not discovery of a
true self" — "I choose to be" states it, "I am" enacts it. Given `Global Drafting
Risks` says *prefer missing speeches to translated symbolism*, "I am" is the one
that trusts the reader. But it is your last sentence, so it is your call. `{TK}`

## What is genuinely fine, so you don't go looking

- **Time scale.** Five to eight weeks external, stated in two places and
  consistent. Arc 3's dilation is subjective and does not conflict.
- **Vesper's age.** His sheet has him 13 at the Flare, five years back — he is 18,
  which is what his seed asks for. (I previously listed this as unreconciled. It
  was already right.)
- **The volcano and the water loop.** The caldera is the reservoir, the loop is
  both infrastructure and the route to it, and breaking it causes the eruption as
  physical consequence rather than cosmic approval. Clean.
- **Nimbus's role.** Contractor under Ministry contract, never a replacement
  state, appearing through logos and crates with no named executive. Clean.
- **The city's decline.** Specified as cleaner schedules, quieter districts,
  thinner weather. That is a usable instruction, not atmosphere.

## Correction to hole 1 — "what do the goggles do" is the wrong question

`World/Magic System` answers it already, and I missed it on the first pass:

> **"The Imprint does not contain a preset power. The Deviation belongs to the
> person and is unique to that bonded relationship."**
>
> `passive resonance → compatible-Imprint pull → bonded unique Deviation`

So the goggles do nothing. **Rain** does the Clone, and it is his, not theirs. The
sheet was never missing a power; I was asking for one the system forbids.

The real gap is one step back, and the rules state the exact shape of it:

> **"Resonance is strongest when an Imprint's completed transition and a person's
> unresolved transition share an emotional or behavioral shape."**

So the question is: **what irreversible transition patterned these goggles, and
how does its shape match Rain's unresolved one?** His unresolved transition is
documented — he quit a stable path irreversibly and it did not change him. His
Deviation is the Clone: borrowed motion, a body acting in his place.

Whatever completed around the goggles has to rhyme with *a body doing the work
with nobody in it.*

### What the prose has already settled

- **The Pull is written, and it is rules-correct.** *"Something was pulling my mind
  here. My attention snapped toward something in the room and refused to settle
  anywhere else."* Compatibility, not instruction. Exactly as defined.
- **Card 0 uses the word first, in the ordinary sense:** *"My gaze wandered hoping
  to find some sort of pull to attract my attention. But I knew this was a
  distraction."* Whether or not that was deliberate, it is a plant. He asked for a
  pull one scene before the Pull arrives.
- **The vault is a lost-and-found**, not an artifact vault — *"You also lost in the
  lost-and-found?"*, bins of *"keys, utility tools, articles of clothing"* sorted by
  ID number. That is a better and more frightening idea than the documents have,
  and it links to hole 3: **corrected people stop initiating, so they never come
  back for their things.** A lost-and-found that only fills up. `World/Locations`
  should be updated to match the prose, not the reverse.
- The goggles are handmade and jury-rigged — skimmer goggles, taped, wires, tiny
  screens, found in a scrap box of half-built tools.

### Three legal answers, by shape

**(a) The worker who kept showing up.** Someone refused inwardly for years and
never stopped performing the job, then walked out mid-shift and never came back for
their things. The completed transition is a refusal that was lived long before it
was acted on. Shape match is the closest available: a body at work with nobody in
it, which *is* the Clone. Fits the drafted whiteboard of chores nobody reads. Cost:
the goggles are Shiori-made, so her building them and someone else's refusal
patterning them are two separate events — legal (Imprints accumulate layers) but
needs one sentence.

**(b) Shiori's own refusal.** She made them, and the transition was hers: she quit
something and they were materially involved. Shape match: her seed makes her the
one who creates without waiting for permission, so her refusal was of permission
itself — the thing Rain cannot give himself. Benefit: ties the inciting object to
the character who carries the book's answer, explains why she is in that vault, and
gives her a reason to want them back that is not plot. Cost: risks making Rain's
Deviation feel derived from hers rather than his own.

**(c) A refusal that was taken.** The worker refused, was corrected for it, and the
goggles were on them when it happened. The completed transition is the refusal
being removed. Shape match: Rain's fear that a finished attempt becomes permanent
evidence. Cost: makes his theft morally heavier, and collides with Arc 4's amnesty
reading them as corporate asset recovery.

`{TK goggles: which completed transition patterned them}` — the author's call.

## Hole 1 — RESOLVED 2026-11-05

**Decision: (a) the worker who kept showing up, recorded by a device of Shiori's.**

Written into the package, verified by reading each document back:

| Document | Change | Words |
|---|---|---|
| `World/Magic System` | New `#### The Goggles` subsection under Imprints | 2935 → 3234 |
| `Characters/Shiori` | "The goggles are her work" bullet expanded; one new Do Not Flatten guard | 1207 → 1285 |
| `World/Locations` | Nimbus Imprint Vault rewritten as a lost-and-found, matching the drafted prose | 655 → 716 |
| `Arc 1 · 2 The vault` | Brief and the body's `%%` note both name the refusal | 2417 → 2430 |
| `Story Plan/Open Questions` | Three answered/void questions removed, two real ones added | 527 → 534 |

### What is now canon

The goggles are early work of Shiori's, a sibling of the recording system she
built after her injury — a bookmark you can wear. A worker's refusal patterned
them afterward: someone who refused inwardly for years and never once stopped
showing up, then walked out mid-shift and never came back for their things.

Two events, not one. She made them; someone else's completed act patterned them.
Imprints accumulate layers, so this needs no exception — and it is why she can own
the object without owning what it became.

The shape match is exact and it is the point: *a body doing the work with nobody
in it* is the Imprint's completed shape and the Clone's whole nature. Rain's own
unresolved transition is the same shape from the other side — he left irreversibly
and it did not change him.

The incidental elegance: the system patterns objects **materially involved** in a
transition, and a device whose entire function is to hold what happened around it
is the most materially involved thing in a room. Her recorder is the ideal Imprint
substrate by accident of its purpose. She has no passive, Imprint or Deviation of
her own.

### Four consequences, all from documents that already existed

1. **Why she is in that vault stops being plot.** The state confiscates her work —
   already canon from the recording system.
2. **"Rain wears her decisions" goes literal twice** — the made object, and the
   decision-memory target Vesper takes.
3. **Vesper takes decisions; Shiori's machines keep them.** She is the structural
   opposite of the antagonist with nothing newly invented.
4. **The vault is a lost-and-found that only fills up**, because corrected people
   stop initiating and never come back for their things. The documents now match
   the drafted prose rather than the reverse.

### The guard

From her own sheet: *"Do not make her connective tissue for Rain, Claire, and
Roxana."* Her interest in the goggles is hers — a piece of her memory system the
state took — never "she made him possible." That is now written into Do Not
Flatten so it cannot drift.

### Left open, deliberately

`{TK goggles: whether the worker is ever identified}` — in `World/Magic System` and
in Open Questions. Anonymity keeps the object's history unlookupable, in a book
whose antagonist's whole advantage is a complete record. Your call.

`{TK}` what the other object is, the one she actually breaches the vault to
recover. No longer load-bearing.
