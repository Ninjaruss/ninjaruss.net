This is the opposite situation from Rain. `Characters/1 Vesper` does not merely
satisfy the seed — on two points it is **more developed than the seed is**, and the
seed's central formulation is already in canon almost word for word:

| Seed | Already in `Characters/1 Vesper` |
|---|---|

**Where the sheet goes beyond the seed.** §3 describes the mother's abandonment. The
sheet supplies what the seed does not: she **deliberately left a prism on the table**

with. His power's origin object is the thing she left instead of staying. The seed
has the wound; the sheet has the wound *and* the mechanism.

Second: **"Removal is his only offer."** Extraction takes, Melt destroys, correction
takes wanting. "He cannot help anyone carry anything… his kindness and his violence
are the same gesture." And **Shatter is the one non-subtractive thing he could do,
and the one thing he withholds.** That is a sharper statement of §4's error than §4
makes.

So the useful output is four findings, not a rewrite.

## §1 WORKED THROUGH 2026-11-05 — the climax chain

Proposal, not written into the package. The placement needs the author (cards cannot
be created by the tool); the chain below is the part to approve or reject first.

### The problem with the gap, stated precisely

Canon's card 7 is: Rain returns from the void and immediately executes a successful
trick. Nothing is risked between the return and the win, and the brief calls him
"injured" with no card injuring him. The seed's steps 4 and 5 are the missing cause.

### The chain, and why each link is forced rather than chosen

The seed's step 4 says Vesper "predicts Rain correctly again" and Rain "attempts an
**expected manoeuvre**." The documents already say what that manoeuvre is.

1. **Step 1 — the Perfect Clone overwhelms him.** It works for one reason: Vesper has
   not yet committed to reading it. An intangible counterfeit that simulates impact is
   unanswerable until you are willing to walk through it. Rain is winning with the
   thing `Themes and Motifs` calls "perfected stasis, not growth."
2. **Step 2 — the cost arrives.** Already canon, card 5: "He knows the correct response
   and begins it too late." The relapse that was winning is what delivers the contact.
   The extraction is the bill for step 1, not bad luck.
3. **Step 3 — the void, the return.** Already canon, cards 6–7.
4. **Step 4 — he reaches for the Perfect Clone again, and Vesper walks through it.**
   This is the expected manoeuvre, because it is the move that has worked all arc, and
   because Vesper is now holding **every decision Rain ever made**. The counterfeit
   was always a bluff that force passes through. Vesper ignores it, reaches the real
   body, and Rain is badly hurt. His own relapse is used against him by a man carrying
   the complete record of it.
5. **Step 5 — the smile.** Between the beating and the last move. §2 above supplies
   what it means: the asylum patient's smile that angered him for no reason he could
   name, arriving now **with everything still in it** — the fear, the failure, the
   un-owned past, the Split he is about to refuse to lose. Vesper has manufactured that
   expression dozens of times by subtraction and has never seen it arrive on its own.
6. **Steps 6–7 — the reversal, and it is already in canon waiting for this.**
   Spine card 7: Rain sets out **the ordinary Clone** as "the expected target," and
   "Vesper commits to the copy while the injured real Rain takes the consequential
   position, receives the blow, and catches him."

   The sharpening this chain provides is *why* Vesper commits to the copy after just
   learning to ignore projections. Because his record is accurate: `Characters/0 Rain`
   — Rain "outsources movement to the Ghost, decisions to Claire, direction to Roxana,
   and **physical presence to the Clone**." Every decision in the prism is a decision
   to stay out of consequence. So Vesper reads the figure in the safe position as the
   real Rain and the one standing in the open as the counterfeit, and he is wrong by
   exactly one decision — the one that is not in the record.

   Not a trick. The same trick, performed by someone who has changed what he is
   willing to stand in.

### Why this is the version to take

- It makes Vesper's correctness last longer and reach further. His final act of
  accurate prediction is currently the extraction; here he is still right about Rain
  *after* the return, and loses anyway.
- It denies the return any reward, which both seeds require.
- It supplies the injury canon already assumes.
- It makes `Arc 4 · 9`'s relapse pay off twice: once as the cause of the extraction,
  once as the move Vesper is ready for.
- Nothing is invented. Every link is a sentence already in Rain's sheet, Vesper's
  sheet, Themes, or the Spine.

### What I am genuinely unsure about

Whether step 4 should teach Vesper "ignore the projection" (making step 6 a reversal
of that lesson) or something else entirely. The reversal is clean, and a reader may
find it *too* clean — a two-step feint rather than a change of character. The
alternative is that step 4 teaches him nothing new and is simply Rain being correctly
predicted and beaten, with step 6 working for the reason canon already gives. Less
elegant, less at risk of reading as a puzzle. Author's call. `{TK}`

## §3 WORKED THROUGH 2026-11-05 — consent at scale

### The mechanism — written into `World/Locations` (716 → 966 words)

Vesper asks permission every time and means it. The city was never asked, and he did
not abandon the practice — **he scaled it through the only instrument the state has.**
At household scale, asking permission is a form.

The *Household Flow Assessment* already existed in the world documents and already
means consent at household scale. It is delivered, read, filed, returned. It says
*stabilization* and *adjustment*, and it does not need to lie, because nobody reading
it is being deceived about water.

This also closes a live seam. `Arc 4 · 5` has Claire asserting "the correction asks
permission" while `Magic System` delivers it through the water supply, which nobody
consents to. Both are true: it asks the way a utility asks, of a household, in
writing, with a deadline. **Her sentence is accurate and it is not an answer** — and
she is neither lying nor naive in saying it.

It is Vesper's error made civic. He treats present authorization as sufficient to
settle what a person may later become; the Ministry collects present authorization at
a rate of one page per address. His virtue survived intact and became the delivery
mechanism.

`{TK}` whether the Assessment is opt-in, opt-out, or carries an objection window
nobody uses. That materially changes how damning it is. Author's call, recorded in
`World/Locations`.

**Never let anyone articulate this.** Vesper does not explain it, Claire does not
concede it, no official defends it. `Do Not Flatten`: "Keep the distinction active in
what he does, not as a speech he loses."

### Placement — three options, author's choice

The register is fixed by `Themes and Motifs`: "Evil arrives through offers, forms,
referrals, renovations, and schedules," and "prefer missing speeches to translated
symbolism." So the form should arrive as **an object somebody has already dealt
with**, never as an argument.

**(a) `Arc 4 · 4 The reveal` — the last call. Recommended.** The brief already reads
"Behind it, unremarked, the parents' district corrects; the phone goes quiet; Rain
mistakes it for relief." Before the calls stop, his mother mentions the notice as
housekeeping — *we got the letter about the water, your father filled it in.* Nothing
else. Her voice already exists on the page: `Arc 1 · 3`'s drafted argument has her
telling him to stay out of trouble and respect the life they want for him. The people
who equated safety with love received a form offering stabilization and complied,
because complying is what they do. It costs two sentences and no speech from anyone.
*Cost:* the card's main event is Roxana's confession, so this risks crowding a scene
that is already carrying something heavier.

**(b) `Arc 4 · 8 The unsigned offer` — the pay-off.** That card already ends with
Rain's own amnesty paperwork unsigned on the table. A *signed* Household Flow
Assessment from his parents' address beside it: one form signed by people who trusted
the system, one left unsigned by their son. *Cost:* very neat. It states the parallel
rather than letting it sit, and it may read as authored.

**(c) `Arc 4 · 5 The advocate` — not needed.** Claire's claim becomes true the moment
the form exists, so the seam closes without a new beat here. Putting the form in her
argument would turn it into a debate about consent, which is what the drafting risk
warns against.

**Recommendation: plant in (a), pay off in (b), change nothing in (c).** Together they
give the escalation a cause, a document and a consequence without anybody explaining
it, and they make `Arc 4 · 8`'s "amputation, not peace" land retroactively — his
parents consented to it.
