## 3. The one structural adjustment the seed asks for

**§6's ethical boundary has no scene.**

The seed is explicit, and it is the only place the seed constrains Rain rather than
freeing him:

> "Making a decision doesn't automatically make that decision good. The ability to
> choose matters, but so do the consequences of our choices for other people."
> … "Agency isn't absolute control."

`Pessimism of Strength` encodes the *other* half of this — care's limit is another
person's ability to participate — but that is Vesper's error, not Rain's. Nothing
currently tests whether **Rain's** agency costs anyone who did not get a vote.

The slot exists and is empty. `Arc 4 · 8 The unsigned offer`: Rain refuses an
amnesty that would resolve the charges, and the cost that lands is *his* — his home
station is corrected hours later. But the boundary was always going to reach that
station; the timing is juxtaposition, not consequence. He pays nothing for the
refusal that he was not already going to pay.

Meanwhile Roxana, Claire and Shiori are on that train. If the amnesty touches their
exposure at all, his refusal is a decision he makes *for* them.

**Recommendation.** Let the refusal bind someone else. Not a betrayal and not a
rebuke — just a cost he chose for a person who would have chosen differently, left
unresolved. Arc 4's stated development is "**Choice does not validate itself**," and
right now that line is asserted in `Arc Structure` and dramatized nowhere. This is
also the one thing that keeps the book's philosophy from reading as
self-permission, which §14 explicitly asks you to avoid: *"I don't want to use that
ideal as another standard for judging someone's worth."* A philosophy of
participation that never costs a bystander anything is a cheaper philosophy than
the one in this document.

Open, because it is a story decision and yours: **whose cost, and does Rain ever
learn of it?** `{TK}`

> now. I've made space, time, and plans … And now that those possibilities are in

`Arc 1 · 4 The drift` is the card where Rain has already made the irreversible

The card currently ends on `{TK the "not yet" in this room}`, which is the one thing

§2 answers it. In card 0 the "not yet" was the story outline he would not open. In

batch is.

Worth noting what this fixes: your §8 Arc 1 summary has Rain take *one*
irreversible step. The drift is where the story admits that the step did not work —
which is the honest version, and the version you have actually lived.

## 5. One thing to confirm, because the seed uses the older term

The seed says Vesper extracts "**autobiographical memories**" (§5, §8, §10).
Yesterday you approved narrowing his hands to **decision memory** only.

These are not in conflict so much as the seed predates the change — but it matters
because the seed describes the *effect* with precision, and decision-only extraction
produces that effect better than the broader version does. If what is taken is every
choice with the weight that attached to it, then what remains is "that happened" with
"I did that" removed. That is §8's "he still knows the story" and §1's "treats each
decision as evidence of who he is," subtracted. The narrowing serves the seed; the
seed's wording just has not caught up.

No change needed unless you disagree — but the seed is the senior document, so you
should be the one to say so.

## 7. The one thing the seed has that no document does

§2's discovery, stated nowhere in the package:

> "Not every experience is extraordinary. Not everything goes well. But some of
> these ordinary experiences have become some of the most meaningful moments I've
> had. **And I didn't necessarily know beforehand that they would matter so much.**"

The story has a great deal about ordinariness as *threat* — the fear of being
ordinary is Rain's engine, and `0 Rain` forbids him from naming it. It has nothing
about ordinariness as **where meaning actually arrived.**

Arc 3's optional lives are the obvious candidate and they are the wrong one: they
are hypothetical, and the arc's job is to end them. What is missing is a small real
moment, not framed as significant when it happens, that turns out to have been one.

I am not recommending a card for this. It is the kind of thing that wants to be
noticed in retrospect rather than built, and §15 says the story should challenge
these ideas rather than prove them. But it is the one genuine absence, and it is
the half of the seed your own life supplied most recently.
