\#\# Rain

A young man with a notebook full of first lines and no second ones.

\#\#\# Quick Read

\- \*\*Age:\*\* 23

\- \*\*Role:\*\* protagonist; the Paralyzed Dreamer

\- \*\*Traits:\*\* \*\*Soft Yearning\*\* / \*\*Sudden Resolve\*\*

\- \*\*Contradiction:\*\* he genuinely wants to discover what he is capable of, yet fear of consequences and a declining belief in his own worth keep him from the experiences through which he could find out.

\- \*\*Question:\*\* \*Can I live according to what matters to me without knowing what it will amount to or who I will become?\*

Rain wants to discover his limits and what he is capable of through living, making, and trying. He can recognize what he wants and understand what pursuing it requires, yet still struggle to act. He treats an immediate attempt as though it must commit his whole future and justify the resources he gives it. Research and planning promise to make him ready; he struggles to recognize when further preparation is postponing an experience he could enter, learn from, and respond to.

After an imperfect attempt, disappointment comes first. Only gradually can he recognize that he made a decision and tried despite being unready. He often discounts real efforts because they have not produced the life or capability he hoped for, making the next attempt harder.

Preserving possibility also protects him from closing other lives and from the fear that a finished attempt will prove he is ordinary. That remains a secondary, unresolved insecurity: he does not yet know how much he wants to find his limits and how much he hopes they will confirm he is exceptional. He never diagnoses that fear aloud; the stopped notebook supplies evidence he cannot comfortably explain.

He grew up praised for potential and steered away from risking it. His hardworking parents equate safety with love: his father tends to decide what Rain means before listening, while his mother offers sincere encouragement without understanding the specific dream. They see extensive computer time, a weaker visible work trajectory, and little finished evidence. Their interpretation is incomplete, not wholly false: Rain genuinely creates, learns, and tries, but reflection and preparation often substitute for irreversible action. Unfinished work and an uncertain future begin to feel like evidence that he has nothing to offer. He learned to deflect rather than explain himself. Their concern is real, but being supported and being seen have never felt identical to him. He wants their understanding, while needing to learn that wanting something does not require their validation.

Before the story, Rain left a stable, approved path because he feared that staying would become indefinite continuation. He quit without a secure replacement, hoping the irreversible leap would force him toward the creative life he imagined. It did not. He still struggled to follow through, finish imperfect work, and produce evidence of the life he claimed to want. Under sufficient local resonance, that exit patterned the personal resonance he later experiences as the Ghost.

\#\#\# On the Page

\- Agreeable, self-deprecating, and easy company; often a fraction absent from his own body.

\- Spins a pen over this notebook, where ideas stop after one line because a second line would become evidence. The stopped pages do not erase his real efforts elsewhere.

\- Looks at ceilings and cloud cover rather than open sky.

\- Struggles when asked to explain a consequential personal choice, even when he knows the answer but finds it difficult to act on. In emergencies, he can move with sudden commitment.

\- Wants to be honest and open with people he feels comfortable around. Sharing a difficulty can still be easier than making a concrete request for help.

\- Carries himself as though his weight has not committed to the next step. Under pressure, that softness snaps into decisive motion.

His generosity and concern for others are real. He yearns for deep connection while considering how much he asks another person to carry. He can explain what holds him back without being able to overcome it through explanation alone.

He enjoys finding people who are driven to make or do things and turn their ambitions into reality; he has felt the lack of that company. Their enthusiasm and activity are attractions in themselves, without every encounter having to become a comparison with his own progress.

\#\#\# Want, Fear, and Change

Rain wants to create a great story capable of inspiring someone as deeply as formative stories inspired him. He wants the experience through which he could discover whether and how he can do it, even while fearing the answer. Can he still respect himself after reality replaces fantasy with evidence? That question remains, but it sits within the larger difficulty of pursuing what matters without advance certainty, approval, or proof of exceptional worth.

A normal, safe life has real attractions. The conflict is whether he chooses it or accepts it because other people and circumstances have already decided who he should be. He cannot fully explain what he hopes to find, but fears never having the chance to find out. Accepting a limitation discovered through experience is different from accepting a prescribed identity before he has tested it; neither requires discovering a true or final self.

As Rain participates, his world grows larger and he feels more alive, even as his social standing and external stability deteriorate. He doubts that feeling when the visible evidence suggests he is throwing his life away. Meaningful experience does not cancel what he loses, and feeling alive does not prove a decision was good. His confidence can falter while his capacity to respond grows.

He outsources movement to the Ghost, decisions to Claire, direction to Roxana, and physical presence to the Clone. His change is learning to commit himself to the choice in front of him, stay involved through difficulty, and try again without first committing every resource or becoming fully prepared. He accepts consequences, reassesses, and pivots while fear, limitation, relapse, and uncertainty remain. Agency includes admitting a mistake, changing a plan, and answering for harm to others; it does not require defending every past decision or rejecting another person's better judgment. Self-sufficiency includes asking for and accepting help without making someone else responsible for choosing his life. He can want without being vindicated, and continue without becoming certain.

1\. He follows the Ghost off his commute and steals the goggles.

2\. Vesper offers relief from wanting; Rain answers \*not yet\*.

3\. He lives and ends his possible lives, including the beloved one.

4\. He refuses the amnesty while it is still available, then relapses through the Perfect Clone.

5\. Vesper successfully extracts every decision Rain ever made. Rain returns with the whole record intact and vivid — rooms, faces, weather, what was said — and no instant anywhere in it where he was the one deciding. The events remain, the authorship does not. He reads his own story rather than remembering living it.

6\. After extraction, he uses the ordinary Clone as the expected target while taking the consequential position and real blow himself, then refuses Vesper's offer to remove the Split.

7\. Still publicly a fugitive, he boards an outbound train toward a chosen first destination, a new adventure, and the next step toward his dreams.

The completed adventure remains a story Rain knows intimately and can recount precisely but cannot inhabit in the first person. The train does not begin restoring that connection. What remains available is his ability to make new consequences through present choices.

Arc 3 made the record of a life and the ownership of having chosen it unusually separable, by forcing him to live, complete, and subsequently know whole lives as stories. The Perfect Clone strengthened the same fracture by returning accurate outcome without felt accomplishment. This residue is not a shield or immunity: Vesper still takes the entire decision target. What it means is that Rain has held an un-owned complete life before, four times, on purpose — so he recognises the state he is in rather than being equipped to resist it.

\#\#\# With Others

\- \*\*Vesper:\*\* Rain is slow, uncertain, and still capable of surprising himself. Vesper decides what a person is from finished evidence and intervenes before they can become otherwise.

\- \*\*Claire:\*\* Rain lets her take decisions from him. In Arc 4 he must answer her in a quiet room rather than converting her certainty into his choice.

\- \*\*Roxana:\*\* he mistakes her usefulness for effortless strength. She gives him home and direction, and needs to be needed without saying so.

\- \*\*Shiori:\*\* she makes imperfect things and lets them exist. Rain admires this without identifying it as the answer to his notebook.

\- \*\*The Pursuer:\*\* Rain postpones attempts when they feel like commitments of his whole future, protecting possibilities he has not tested. The Pursuer cannot stop because he protects an accumulated past.

\#\#\# The Ghost, Clone, Perfect Clone, and Split

Shared passive, Imprint, Deviation, and cost rules belong to \`World/Magic System.md\`. For Rain on the page:

\- \*\*The Ghost:\*\* his private passive, a faded-gold figure with an open, upward palm. It points toward what he wants, never toward a correct method. It grants commitment, not competence, and remains interpretable as hallucination.

\- \*\*The goggles:\*\* Shiori built them as ordinary technology. A worker later used them during a resonant Sacred Water infrastructure shift and finally quit a dead-end job rather than return indefinitely. They retain the completed shape of choosing an unfinished, self-directed future over a prescribed route; Rain's unresolved version of the same transition produces the Pull.

\- \*\*The Clone:\*\* one completely intangible projection at a practical mid-range. It can reproduce Rain's intended voice and projected sound without strict line-of-sight, but it cannot scout, relay sensation, or improvise as an independent mind. Rain must know enough about the destination and intended action to author its scripted behavior. Cost: disrupts sensory anchoring; compounds with length and complexity (see \`World/Magic System.md\`).

\- \*\*The Perfect Clone:\*\* the same intangible counterfeit rendered with convincing physical fidelity. It locally registers enough of its surroundings to simulate rain, impact, wounds, recoil, clothing damage, and incidental sound on the projected body while matter and force pass through it; semantic speech remains authored by Rain. It cannot create unrelated consequences, transmit force, or give Rain access to what it registers. Repeated use temporarily compounds friction in Rain's want → commit → act pathway: he retains desire and knowledge but loses felt authorship and struggles to initiate action. The dimming Ghost is a symptom of that recoverable impairment, not a finite resource being consumed.

\- \*\*The Split:\*\* Rain's record of past failure in his own voice. Before the mountain it is unmarked and wears the shape of whichever life Rain is inside, so he meets it as a replica of himself rather than as a voice; the copies it wore are what the mountain holds. After the mountain it is marked and italicized. Never settle whether it is another entity or only Rain.

Ordinary Clone and Perfect Clone remain alternate expressions, one projection at a time. Before extraction, Perfect Clone works well enough that its accumulated initiation cost helps Vesper obtain contact: Rain knows the response and begins it too late. After Vesper removes every decision attached to the goggles — including the choice to take them — the resonant bond remains. Semantic and procedural knowledge let Rain use the ordinary Clone for the decisive decoy even though he no longer remembers learning it.

\#\#\# Visual Anchors

Rain looks ordinary and approachable rather than visibly heroic. Keep the notebook, pen, and later goggles. His visual distinction comes from the contrast between hesitant everyday posture and sudden emergency movement. He never perceives his own shine; show it once from outside. Avoid physical dominance or unusually heroic styling at the beginning.

\#\#\# Do Not Flatten

\- His abilities complicate or expose his choices; they do not make him competent enough to bypass them.

\- Neither Clone is tangible, autonomous, or a source of remote sensory information.

\- Keep his body acting while the Split argues. Other characters do not resolve the scene for a frozen Rain.

\- He does not discover a true or final self. He remains hesitant, unfinished, and capable of relapse.

\- Keep fear of being ordinary as a secondary insecurity he has not resolved and does not diagnose aloud. Let his stopped notebook carry it without making it the sole explanation for his hesitation.

\- Do not treat a safe life as inherently lesser, every refusal as agency, or every consequence as proof that others have misunderstood him. Wanting does not excuse harm; accepting accountability need not mean accepting an externally prescribed identity.

\- Do not equate surrender to the authorities with surrender of agency. What returning would concretely require him to give up in Arc 1 remains unresolved; do not invent a condition to make his refusal automatically right.

\- His departure is neither public vindication nor exile presented as freedom. The fugitive condition remains; choosing a direction within it is the change.

\- Do not imply that boarding the train restores felt ownership. The choice matters because he makes it without that reward.
