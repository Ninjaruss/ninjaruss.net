## Shiori

A tinkerer who made one very good thing, lost it to the state, and kept making imperfect things anyway.

### Snapshot

- **Age:** 25–26
- **Height:** 5'8"
- **Role:** maker, recorder, and the fourth front of the finale
- **Contradiction:** she cannot reliably hold her own life and is the most present person in any room.
- **Question:** *If I cannot preserve the thing itself, can I trust what it changed?*

Shiori asked Vesper for help once and was damaged by an early, imprecise extraction. She never remembers making that request. Since then, she has trusted machines rather than people to hold her place.

Stress worsens her memory condition; calm routines help stabilize it. Her narration, deployed tools, backups, and repeated work habits are accommodations rather than decorative eccentricities.

### On the page

- Fast, cheerful, kinetic, and always using her hands.
- Narrates her work aloud as a memory aid disguised as chatter.
- Leaves each tool on the exact seam where she stopped. The tool is a bookmark, not clutter.
- Marks her work with a doodle, charm, or boot-screen joke. Never a formal logo.
- Builds redundancy into small objects because she is bracing for the next loss.
- Occasionally freezes to recover her place, then continues without self-pity.

Her maker's marks are playful. Her survival system is not. Keep that gap visible.

### Conflict

Shiori is not trying to recover a lost past. She is defending a future self against another disappearance. Building is also how she avoids asking what she actually needs.

She will not ask a person to remember for her. Her Arc 5 wager is small: she closes something without marking her place and trusts she can return.

What she would not admit is that she wants accompaniment without having to earn it through usefulness. Keep this beneath her connection with Rain; it is not a romance endpoint or a cure.

The story never treats her original request as wrong. She asked the one person for whom asking was dangerous.

### Relationships and contrasts

- **Vesper:** he corrects and replaces; she patches and accepts the seam. His work removes provenance. Hers leaves the hand visible.
- **Rain:** he protects possibility by stopping before the second line. She signs unfinished weekend jank and lets the making count. Her activity does not make her his cure.
- **Claire:** Shiori is material, improvised, and visibly lived-in where Claire is clean, controlled, and systematic.
- **Roxana:** both provide and struggle to ask. Roxana needs her absence to matter; Shiori fears losing the record that proves she was there.

### Work, equipment, and cost

Shiori has no passive, Imprint, or Deviation. That absence is not a measure of human capacity or worth.

Her first major project was an external recording system built after her injury. The state confiscated and scaled it into unattended surveillance infrastructure. The same system later preserves evidence everyone else ignored.

The goggles are her work. Rain wears her decisions from Arc 1 onward before knowing who made them.

Most of her other devices are handmade, patched, and specific to a problem. In Arc 5 she burns out her own rig component by component to maintain feeds across every front. This is a chosen material cost, not a surprise failure.

### Arc

1. She appears unnamed in the vault, taking back confiscated work, and remembers Rain's nervous laugh.
2. Her equipment and maker's mark recur before she joins the cast.
3. She boards the Circuit Line to accuse Rain. Her own recordings exonerate him, and she stays.
4. She carries the knowledge that the system saving them is also the system watching the city.
5. She runs the finale's fourth front and spends the rig that has served as an archive of herself.
6. She turns the cameras off, puts a tool away instead of leaving it as a bookmark, and keeps Rain's laugh without notes.

### Visual anchors

Shiori is wiry, kinetic, hands-busy, and slightly improvised. Tools, functional patches, handmade modifications, and maker traces matter more than exact fashion. She should look like someone who occupies space by doing things in it. Avoid decorative “quirky engineer” clutter with no functional reason.

### Guards

- She is never cured and does not need a refusal scene.
- Learning what Vesper did must affect her; continuing does not mean she is untouched.
- She never states the theme or explains what memory means.
- Her goggles and recorder are made objects with compromises, not neutral gear.
- Keep one quiet beat of complicity about the surveillance system. Do not turn it into a guilt speech.
- Her connection with Rain remains an allusion, not a reward, cure, confession, kiss, or genre-signaled romance.
- In Arc 5 she does not reassure Rain that his memories are his. She simply continues beside him without treating his condition as an emergency.
