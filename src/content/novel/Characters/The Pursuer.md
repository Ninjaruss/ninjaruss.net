## The Pursuer

A man who has served the same institution for thirty years and calculated the date he runs empty.

### Snapshot

- **Age:** roughly 50–55
- **Height:** 6'2"
- **Role:** institutional pursuer and Rain's strongest foil
- **Contradiction:** nothing can stop him, and he wants nothing.
- **Question:** *Is it too late to stop?*

His career predates the Flare. His supernatural condition does not. Thirty years of service supplied the heading; the last five years turned endurance and wanting into fuel.

### On the page

- Unhurried, courteous, minimal, and procedural.
- Never runs, at any point, regardless of anyone else's speed.
- Completes records and signs them.
- Does not lie or misstate an offer.
- Hurts only as much as the assigned task requires.
- Steam appears in scene corners before the reader understands it belongs to him.

He knows exactly what has happened to him. His tragedy is not ignorance but the fact that he has spent all his endurance continuing and none of it leaving.

### Conflict

The Pursuer mistakes endurance for identity. Stopping appears to invalidate the person who spent thirty years continuing.

He once wanted to find people moving toward the same thing with equal force. He never asked for that life. The institution supplied a direction instead, and eventually the borrowed heading became the only self he could recognize.

### Relationships and contrasts

- **Rain:** Rain wants without acting. The Pursuer acts without wanting. Rain fears losing possible futures; the Pursuer fears betraying the accumulated past.
- **Vesper:** the Pursuer surrenders choice to process. Vesper claims choice over everyone else. One obeys the institution's verb; the other cannot wait for institutions or people.
- **Roxana:** he can continue one old decision until the fuel ends. She can stop, hurt, and choose to relight.

Rain asks him whether it is too late to stop during the Arc 4 amnesty. The steam reacts once. Nobody identifies the connection later.

### Passive, Deviation, and cost

**Burning / furnace:** the wanting he once carried becomes internal heat and inexhaustible-looking motion.

**The greatcoat:** decades-old field wool that became his resonant Imprint after the Flare. It traps and shapes his vapor into a boundary layer that turns blades and flash-boils rain.

The armor is steam, not plate. Plate armor is the city's myth of him.

Every defense and completed task burns more of what remains. He wins exchanges by emptying himself. Running empty means he finally stops walking.

### Arc

1. Steam and a distant figure give the hunt a face.
2. In round one, he loses only the exit and does not chase beyond the task.
3. In round two, paperwork and procedure defeat the group without a raised hand.
4. His signature appears on Aster's intake record.
5. He presents a genuine amnesty and reads his own disposal date without expression.
6. Against Roxana, he spends ahead of schedule, empties, and stops walking. Steam condenses on cold wet wool.

### Visual anchors

The Pursuer has the cast's largest and heaviest-feeling silhouette. The greatcoat, steam, upright posture, and deliberate walking are essential. He should feel inevitable rather than aggressive. Avoid exaggerated bodybuilder proportions, literal plate armor, or sudden bursts of speed.

### Guards

- He never runs.
- He never lies.
- He never allies, supplies intelligence, joins the heroes, or receives a redemption scene. The gate simply stops being manned.
- The coat does not come off as a cure.
- Roxana does not overpower his endurance; he empties while she repeatedly chooses to return.
- His final stop is unrecorded. Nobody thanks him, and he files nothing.
- Do not turn endurance itself into the answer. Endurance without re-choosing is momentum.
