## The Pursuer

A man who has served the same institution for thirty years and calculated when the Furnace will run empty.

### Quick Read

- **Age:** roughly 50–55
- **Height:** 6'2"
- **Role:** institutional pursuer, practical combat threat, and Rain's strongest foil
- **Traits:** **Procedural Persistence** / **Buried Longing**
- **Contradiction:** nothing can stop him, and he wants nothing.
- **Question:** *Is it too late to stop?*

His career predates the Flare; his supernatural condition does not. Thirty years of service supplied the heading, and the last five years turned endurance and wanting into fuel. He knows the cost, the projected depletion date, and the possibility of stopping. He does not know when he will die. He is a conscious husk of his former self, not a faceless or mindless force that mistakes obedience for innocence.

### On the Page

- Unhurried, courteous, minimal, and procedural.
- Never runs, at any point, regardless of anyone else's speed.
- Completes records and signs them.
- Does not lie or misstate an offer.
- Hurts only as much as the assigned task requires.
- Maintains upright posture and deliberate walking even in combat.
- Arrives first as steam gathering in scene corners before the reader understands it belongs to him.

He knows exactly what has happened to him. His tragedy is not ignorance but the fact that he has spent all his endurance continuing and none of it leaving.

### Want, Fear, and Change

The Pursuer mistakes endurance for identity. Stopping appears to invalidate the person who spent thirty years continuing. He spent those years equating provision with love, supplying his family stability and material support while increasingly replacing presence with work. From his perspective he gave them everything and it was not enough; from theirs he gave them everything except himself.

He once wanted to find people moving toward the same thing with equal force. He never asked for that life. The institution supplied a direction instead, and eventually the borrowed heading became the only self he could recognize.

After the Flare, the family separation became final while he was working in a resonant wet/Sacred Water environment. Given an opportunity to pursue them, return home, grieve, or stop, he accepted another institutional task in his long-used field greatcoat. Choosing work as the replacement for wanting patterned both him and the materially involved coat: his personal resonance became Furnace, while the greatcoat became the Imprint of continuing after the reason for continuation had disappeared.

1. Steam and a distant figure give the hunt a human shape.
2. In round one, he loses only the exit and does not chase beyond the task.
3. In round two, paperwork and procedure defeat the group without a raised hand.
4. His signature appears on Aster's intake record.
5. He presents a genuine amnesty and reads the Furnace's projected depletion date without expression. Rain asks whether it is too late to stop; the steam reacts once.
6. Against Roxana, he spends ahead of schedule, empties, and stops walking. Steam condenses on cold wet wool.

He does not complete a conversion or choose a new side. His final change is the terminal fact that the old direction can no longer carry him.

### With Others

- **Rain:** Rain wants without acting. The Pursuer acts without wanting. Rain fears losing possible futures; the Pursuer fears betraying the accumulated past.
- **Vesper:** the Pursuer surrenders choice to process. Vesper claims choice over everyone else. One obeys the institution's verb; the other cannot wait for institutions or people.
- **Roxana:** he can continue one old decision until the fuel ends. She can stop, hurt, and choose to relight. She does not overpower him; her repeated returns force him to keep paying for the assigned result.
- **Aster:** the Pursuer's signature on Aster's intake record makes him part of the history Roxana inherits. The record matters more than a confession, apology, or explanatory flashback.

### Furnace, Greatcoat, and Finite Fuel

Shared passive, Imprint, Deviation, and cost rules belong to `World/Magic System.md`. For the Pursuer on the page:

- **Furnace:** converts the affective and motivational charge attached to old desires into abnormal heat and continued supernatural capacity. Factual memory remains; what disappears is each remembered want's power to pull him toward another future. He stays rational, perceptive, morally comprehending, and procedurally capable even as less within him can produce *I want something else*.
- **The greatcoat:** decades-old field wool patterned in the same event as Furnace. It captures and shapes vapor produced from his heat and whatever moisture is available. It creates no water: heavy rain is ideal, damp air is functional, and dry conditions restrict it to breath, sweat, retained fabric moisture, and limited humidity.
- **Sheath:** a hot turbulent vapor-and-pressure boundary that obscures close movement, spoils contact, and redirects attacks without behaving like invisible plate armor.
- **Vent:** a short-to-mid-range directed release that can scald, unbalance, break contact, obscure vision, or clear space. It is not artillery.
- **Shape / redirect:** practical redistribution of available vapor for defense, concealment, and approach control. No elaborate constructs or unrestricted heat manipulation.
- **Projectile defense:** directed pressure can alter the path of ordinary handgun and rifle fire. Concentrated fire sharply increases expenditure; very high-energy projectiles and explosions are not automatically negated.
- **Cost:** Furnace consumes a minute amount at baseline and substantially more during pursuit, defense, venting, projectile deflection, or forced continuation through injury and exhaustion. Spent charge does not regenerate. Years of thermal and workload records let him extrapolate a depletion date; it is not prophecy, and heavy use moves it forward.

His combat presence is practical: he advances, takes the shortest sufficient action, uses vapor to control contact and defense, and refuses to spend effort beyond the task. He feels inevitable because he does not hurry, not because he is tactically omnipotent.

He never runs or uses steam for propulsion, launch movement, or sudden dashes. The greatcoat has no advanced expression; this is a fixed character rule, not an undiscovered upgrade. The active Deviation's name remains unresolved.

Against Roxana he spends far ahead of the projected rate. At zero, Furnace permanently ends, abnormal heat vanishes, the steam dissipates, and the greatcoat becomes ordinary wet wool. Age, injury, fatigue, and years of supernatural continuation become fully relevant, and he stops walking. Severe lasting mobility impairment is likely, but the exact degree remains open rather than magically fixed as total paralysis. He may remain conscious.

### Visual Anchors

The Pursuer has the cast's largest and heaviest-feeling silhouette. The greatcoat, steam, upright posture, deliberate walking, and wet wool at the end are essential. He should feel inevitable rather than aggressive. Avoid exaggerated bodybuilder proportions, literal plate armor, or sudden bursts of speed.

### Do Not Flatten

- He never runs and never lies.
- He knows when Furnace is projected to empty, not when he will biologically die.
- Courtesy, restraint, accurate paperwork, and the old unasked desire keep him human without excusing what he does.
- Do not reduce him to a symbolic obstacle. His procedural decisions should have concrete tactical and institutional consequences.
- He never allies, supplies intelligence, joins the heroes, or receives a redemption scene. The gate simply stops being manned.
- The coat does not come off as a cure.
- The coat never creates water, grants propulsion, or develops an advanced form.
- Roxana does not overpower his endurance; he empties while she repeatedly chooses to return.
- Nobody later identifies the steam's reaction to Rain's question.
- His final stop is unrecorded. Nobody thanks him, and he files nothing.
- Endurance is not the answer. Endurance without re-choosing is momentum.
