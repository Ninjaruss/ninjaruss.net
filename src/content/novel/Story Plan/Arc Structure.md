## Arc Structure

This file records what each arc does to the story. `1 The Spine.md` owns scene order and causal detail; `Choice Points.md` owns pitfall behavior.

### Arc Function

| Arc | Primary lens | Structural job | Choice pressure | Handoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Fugitive** | Rain | Break the commute, make Rain's impulsive theft consequential, assemble the first group, and connect the accusation to the asylum. | Take one irreversible step before feeling ready, or go home. | The apparent refuge is pleasant and open-doored. |
| **2 · Mirror** | Vesper | Establish relief that works, then reframe Vesper's genuine care as intervention without informed consent. | Keep wanting when wanting hurts, or become a patient. | Rain and Roxana are referred to the state. |
| **3 · Prisoner** | Rain's interior / Roxana's action | Separate possibility from consequence: Rain ends the beloved life while Roxana accepts help and breaks the prison seal. | End lives that feel real, or remain in one. | Rain carries Roxana toward the Circuit Line. |
| **4 · Witness** | Claire | Remove immediate pursuit long enough for Rain to answer Claire's evidence without asking her to decide, expose the correction's cost, and turn apparent growth into relapse. | Choose before the amnesty expires, or let the loop become home. | The Perfect Clone carries the group toward the caldera under false progress. |
| **5 · Proof** | Ensemble into Rain | Spend the cast's established strengths to clear a path, let extraction fully succeed, and test whether a new response remains possible without accumulated proof. | No pitfall and no ending selector. | Rain remains a fugitive and boards the outbound train by choice. |

### Arc 5 Convergence

- **Central Question:** Can you take one irreversible step before feeling ready?

- **Thematic development:** Action produces consequence. Rain finally does something and it makes his life substantially worse, immediately rejecting risk as an automatic route to reward. His safe life must retain genuine value so leaving it costs something.

- Rain seemingly running away from his problems that have been thrust upon him, but really he’s avoiding the stuff in himself, the introspection. He is forced to be portrayed as a fugitive as a result of a singular small commitment to being uncomfortable (making a decision to change the status quo of his life; aka break the cycle of his commute for a moment to explore the ghost).

- The old life is not merely cowardice or dead weight: family, safety, familiarity, competence, and small pleasures give it value. Leaving therefore creates loss before it creates possibility, and later events never retroactively prove that the first step was correct.

**Decided 2026-08-08 — the pitfall:** *go home.* Card 4 (the drift), exiting to card 5 (the follow). A comfortable loop of fugitive routine where hiding almost works; stay in it and he turns himself in and admits wrongs he didn't commit.

**Decided 2026-08-04 — the inciting incident.** The restricted room is the System's vault of confiscated Imprints; what calls to him is the goggles, and taking them out is the theft. The public charge is the disappearances — Vesper's crimes pinned on him, self-renewing. Family appears as calls and messages only, never on screen, and every one of those calls is load-bearing for Arc 4.

The Ghost reads as hallucination throughout and is never explained in-arc. Ends on the asylum threshold: pleasant, open-doored.

**Arc 2: The Mirror (Asylum)**

- **Owned by:** Vesper. His house, his argument, his four tells; the arc is his case being made honestly.

- **Central Question:** Can you keep wanting when wanting hurts? *(Revised 2026-08-28. The original — "Can you trust someone who seems to be everything you wish you were?" — remains retired as the compass and survives only as a reread question: Vesper is a twist of kind, not of mask.)*

- **Thematic development:** Relief can genuinely help. The Widower's first treatment remains successful. The danger develops through repetition: pain → relief → restored capacity can become pain → intervention → dependence → diminishing ability to determine what comes next. The story is not arguing that suffering is necessary for humanity.

- An abandoned asylum that’s been repurposed as a refuge for the broken. Establishes Vesper’s kindness and showcases how effective his methods (not fully revealed that he is manipulating/removing memories from people). But, it’s actually very dystopian and by the end of the arc, he betrays the group. Likely here he will cause Roxana to be effectively paralyzed (her body loses the memory of being able to stand or have tenacity) and will cause Claire to follow him due to her goals/philosophy (the surefire high probability of solving her existential threat, perhaps a terminal illness or something else, not sure). Both Rain and Roxana are captured.

- The patients/refugees we see in the asylum are people that are happy and seem to feel free. They don’t have any random or defining irrational quirks making them a bit more forgettable. They do stuff to make the asylum more of a sanctuary, but only through tasks and never making things on their own accord. They seem content and are stuck in the bubble that is the asylum.

- **Decided 2026-08-19 — one of them has a name and a file.** The Widower (see Characters/The Widower.md) is on the page here, not just cited in the cure-test decision. He runs the kitchen, is warm to everyone, and tells the story of his wife's death fluently and without feeling — the one deliberate exception to "no defining quirks," since he's the founding case the whole cure test is built on and needs a face to carry that weight.

- A key thing to note is how tragic the actual details of each of the patients we see in the asylum are, but they never seem to be really burdened by it anymore. It should show just how technically correct their removal of the pains/struggles are; that they are seemingly high functioning and do plenty of good now. But what’s lost is subtle, the human core is gone and in a way, it’s like they are NPCs designed to make the asylum look good rather than being actual people building their own stories.

- The question is not whether pain is secretly beautiful or necessary. Treatment can return capacity and should when it does. The danger is an intervention that succeeds so completely and repeatedly that it begins substituting for the person's ability to revise, refuse, want, or decide what comes next.

**Decided 2026-08-03 — what he actually takes.** Mainly the *wanting*, not the trauma. He does also remove genuinely impairing pain, and the Widower's first extraction was unambiguously correct — conceded, no take-backs. The crack is the repetition: it worked so well he came back for the next pain, and the next, nine times. Nothing was taken that he'd have grown from; the growth never happened. Rain's rebuttal never touches the first one — *what about the second?*

Disclosure runs in three layers: the result is open, the mechanism is shown deliberately by Vesper mid-arc, and the terms are never revealed.

**Decided 2026-08-11 — Vesper's core is impatience.** He cannot bear to wait for people to figure it out; the *watching* is the intolerable thing, which is why he runs an institution rather than quietly medicating himself. His question: *how long am I supposed to watch?*

**Decided 2026-08-08 — the pitfall:** *put it down.* Card 6 (the archive), exiting to card 7. Stay and he becomes a patient.

**Arc 3: The Prisoner**

- **Owned by:** Rain's interior — and Roxana's plot. His trial is a void; every *event* in the arc is hers (the tank, the review, the signature, the accepted hand, the spark, the gutter, the carry), and the book's only POV cut is to her. It is Roxana's arc happening around Rain's trial.

- **Central Question:** Can you end every life you could have lived, even the one where you were happy?

- An intrusive and claustrophobic cell with deprivation pods designed to pacify Deviants and troublemakers. Rain lives possible lives whole, including a beloved life that is genuinely happy and meaningful, and then ends them. He does not destroy false selves until an authentic one remains. He relinquishes valuable possibilities because a life preserved from outside consequence cannot continue answering or being answered by anything beyond itself. The Ghost reaches Roxana because she is who Rain wants not to lose; it does not remotely activate her power, choose for her, or grant competence.

**Correction (2026-08-03/06):** the second clause above is wrong for the Ghost — it expresses Rain's internal want and *nothing else*. In the tank it reaches Roxana because she is who he wants not to lose, not because she is nearest.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — the trial is time dilation without a true-self endpoint.** Rain lives each potential life whole, as a dream, then ends it. The mountain of bodies is a graveyard of lived experience and valuable possibility, not a pile of discarded falsity. The beloved life is lived longest and is genuinely happy. He leaves because preserving it indefinitely prevents anything outside it from answering back. Possibility matters; preserved possibility cannot acquire consequences beyond itself.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — the delayed bill.** Living a thousand lives that felt exactly like life teaches his mind to encode lived experience twice: as autobiographical ownership and as a semantic story that remains available after a life is completed. This is not immunity in Arc 5. It is the first condition that lets independently encoded semantic knowledge remain after Vesper successfully extracts the autobiographical target.

**Decided 2026-08-08 — the pitfall:** *stay in the life* — the long movie. Card 3, exiting to card 4.

**Arc 4: The Witness (Circuit Line)**

- **Owned by:** Claire. The advocate and the voice are the arc's spine; Shiori is the arrival, not the owner.

- **Central Question:** Can you choose when nothing is chasing you? *(Revised 2026-08-28; the System tolerates rather than chases.)*

- **Thematic development:** Choice does not validate itself. Rain receives time, evidence, legal amnesty, and a defensible route home. He refuses and still loses his district. Choice is not correctness, reward, or control over consequence; it only makes him a participant in what follows.

- Looping transit to give an opportunity for the party to figure out a plan or what to do. They have arguments on if they should pursue Vesper as on paper he is doing stuff that is truly saving society. Opportunity to reveal parts of each character like what Roxana faced in the deprivation chamber and Claire's secret.

**Decided 2026-08-05 — the endgame register is the infinite commute.** The correction rolls out like an update; decay is always rendered as improvement; the master clock is the boundary creeping one station per lap. No dystopian set dressing, ever.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — three visitors seal three exits.** Shiori boards as an accuser and is flipped by her own confiscated machines. Roxana tells her tank flat. Claire returns to make the honest case *for* Vesper at full strength, and Rain finally answers in a quiet room. The fifty-first try and retry capacity can remain part of his challenge, but they are not a universal theorem the story makes him prove; the arc has already shown that continuing can become momentum and that a chosen act can still be wrong.

**Added 2026-08-19 — the case Claire actually makes.** Written out in full in Themes and Motifs, "Antithesis (Vesper)": the cure test run at civic scale, arguing the waiting room is itself unconsented mass coercion and the correction at least asks. It must land as genuinely strong before Rain's rebuttal — see that doc's guard against strawmanning it.

**The parents' silence.** The boundary takes their district mid-arc and the calls stop. Rain misreads the quiet as relief; at the anchor station — his own home station — he does the arithmetic and understands he mistook amputation for peace.

The Devil's Bargain is the state's amnesty with Rain's goggles on the manifest: burn the vault and un-take the inciting incident. He refuses while the offer is live. The Perfect Clone manifests after the refusal — relapse dressed as reward. The arc ends on false gold.

**Decided 2026-08-08 — the pitfall:** *let it expire.* Card 7 (the amnesty), exiting to card 8.

**Arc 5: The Proof (Caldera)**

- **Owned by:** Rain, with card 9's codas-in-rings as a staged handoff — the arc giving itself away in order, then the outbound train taking it back for one man alone.

- **Central Question:** Can you choose without knowing who that choice proves you are?

- **Thematic development:** Evidence reaches its limit. Vesper obtains the most complete past evidence of Rain possible. It still cannot determine the next response. A completed account of a person cannot contain an event that has not happened yet.

- The group fully commits to chasing down Vesper and stopping him from enacting forced correction onto the populace.

- Final confrontation: Vesper offers to remove the Split. Rain refuses. He sets aside the Clone, writes the first line. The Split whispers. He writes the second line. Triggers a controlled eruption; the water loop breaks; the rain falls clean.

- Roxana fills the lighter with her own flame. Claire makes the final call without the Click. Shiori stays, remembering without notes.

- Vesper is left with the prism, his philosophy cracked.

- Final image: Rain's notebook open, two lines written, the rest blank. *I dreamed and dreamed. Now I fall. In spite of it all, I am Rain.* The name is a present claim and accepted point of departure, not a discovered essence.

**No pitfall.** This is where the loops break.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — three simultaneous fights, one per organ.** Roxana vs the Pursuer (body) is the recurring Battle subplot's local resolution: she relights while he can only spend, and he finally stops. Claire vs the emptied (mind) stages deciding without certainty. Rain vs Vesper (will) reaches the limit of finished evidence: Vesper's understanding of Rain's past can be accurate without possessing the next act.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — why Vesper loses.** His power is composed entirely of other people's finished pasts, and a record is always out of date. The shard of Roxana he predicts Rain with was taken before she learned to stand without the memory of standing. Then direct contact gives him Rain's complete autobiographical past. The extraction fully succeeds; what it does not give him is authority over the response that occurs after the record ends.

**Decided 2026-08-28 — the cost and mechanism.** Rain loses every autobiographical memory-network Vesper targets. Nothing is shielded or secretly retained. Arc 3's completed lives and Arc 4's Perfect Clone have already made semantic knowledge unusually independent from felt ownership, so accurate factual knowledge remains: not “I remember meeting Roxana,” but “Rain met Roxana.” He can recount the adventure perfectly and cannot inhabit it as something that happened to him. Triumph goes light; the ache stays heavy. He never regains ownership.

**Added 2026-08-19 — this cost is payoff, not a new wound.** The Perfect Clone already taught him this trade in Arc 4: send the version of himself that risks nothing, get relief with no weight in it back. The finale is the same trade paid honestly for the first time — he finally commits for real, and the part of him that registers weight has spent the whole book atrophying on substitutes. It is not a twist of fate. It is the bill. (See Rain's character doc, "How he processes," for the Clone-as-potential equation this pays off — never state the connection on the page.)

**Superseded (2026-08-07):** the old betrayal-mid-finale beat, and "Claire may have been assisting Vesper unknowingly" — her complicity is now the delivered patients plus her advocacy, resolved at the door. The trial and mountain material now lives in Arc 3.

#### Key Plot Beats

- Rain sees an afterimage seemingly leading him towards a different path on his commute. This decision alone leads him to the inciting events of the story.

- Vesper seemingly acts differently during certain parts of the story. In terms of foreshadowing: he is forgetful of recent events after a recent memory extraction and will casually react to things based on memories he has already extracted from people. **The four tells, in order (decided 2026-08-06):** a fighting style that isn't his, a gap in what he could know, the loadout at breakfast, and a phrase belonging to Shiori.

- Vesper is revealed to have the ability to extract memories and has done this for his own goals. He betrays the group; extracting Roxana's important decisive memory of her past to cause her to physically be paralyzed. **Decided 2026-08-11:** he takes it because he has correctly diagnosed that her standing is what's killing her — right about the diagnosis, monstrous in the cure.

- **Decided 2026-08-04:** Aster Sparks is dead before the story begins. An investigator who hunted Vesper, became the prototype referral, and died in the deprivation prison. His shard survives in Vesper's loadout; Roxana learns only that it exists; the death reveals at the end of Arc 3.

- Both Rain and Roxana are trapped within the sensory deprivation prison designed to pacify inmates. Rain lives completed possible lives until experience and story become separable forms of record.

- Rain stands bloodied on a mountain of bodies that are all lives he genuinely lived. The image carries the cost of relinquishing possibility; it does not imply that false selves were stripped away to reveal a final one.

- Vesper is practically unbeatable by Rain not only due to having so much accumulated memories inside him, but the fact that Roxana's core memory was extracted, which allows Vesper to fully predict Rain's combat maneuvers as he was primarily trained by Roxana.

- Rain slips and gives Vesper direct contact. Vesper extracts the complete autobiographical target. The void that follows contains exact semantic knowledge without lived ownership and asks only for small present acts after the record ends.

- The void does not reveal a buried self, restore an old one, or let Rain listen to a soul that escaped extraction. Small non-branching acts create new consequences after the finished record. He returns recognizably hesitant, soft, afraid, and unfinished; Vesper predicts the Clone correctly, while the real Rain accepts the blow and catches him himself.
