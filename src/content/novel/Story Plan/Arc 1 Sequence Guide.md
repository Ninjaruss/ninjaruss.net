## Sequence 1 — Taking the First Step

Existing cards: **0 Rain intro → 1 The road less traveled → 2 The vault → 3 The morning after → 4 The charge lands**. No additional scene or prose split is required to group these cards.

1. **Dramatic question:** will Rain act on an attraction he cannot justify, and what happens when that private act becomes public?
2. **Immediate desire:** break the familiar postponement, explore, and keep the goggles he wants. Afterward he wants breathing room, an explanation, and to remain understood by his family. Do not retroactively give the theft a grand mission.
3. **External conflict:** restricted space and security; later public identification and family response. Attraction does not erase ownership or make theft ethically equivalent to quitting a job.
4. **Emotional progression:** longing and self-interruption → curiosity → wanting amid fear → escape and temporary relief → frustration and postponed disclosure → exposure and hurt. The value of feeling alive need not disappear when consequences arrive.
5. **Climax:** taking the goggles is the action commitment. The family call is the emotional turn: the explanation he hoped to have is still unavailable when others demand one.
6. **Payoff/consequence:** he got out with what he wanted, but did not acquire a defensible account of himself. The delay in disclosure can acquire its own relational cost; the exact opportunity to disclose is still a placeholder.
7. **Carry-forward question:** what can he do now, while neither retreat nor continuing feels clearly justifiable? Sequence 2 needs a specific reason to leave home, not an automatic escalation supplied off-page.
8. **Setups:** the security-recording warning anticipates identification, without explaining the disappearance allegation; cyan tape pays off immediately in the escape and remains a trace, not proof clearing him; the nervous laugh has its existing later recognition payoff (`Characters/Shiori:43–47`). Preserve the unfinished notebook for its much later second line (`Themes and Motifs:54`).

Scene intentions and provisional drafting envelopes:
- **0 Rain intro — 400–650 words:** establish a specific creative want and ordinary hesitation. Existing prose retained. Do not turn the opening into an inventory of deficiencies.
- **1 The road less traveled — 500–750:** let attention to the street become the decision to enter the alley. Existing prose retained; no advance explanation of the Ghost.
- **2 The vault — 2,000–2,600:** encounter, wanting, taking, escape, and the return to routine. Existing prose retained; any clearer liberation beat must be authored later.
- **3 The morning after — 500–800:** failed understanding and one consequential postponement of disclosure. Planned beat stays in the existing placeholder; no invented call or lie.
- **4 The charge lands — 700–1,100:** the broadcast and full family exchange collide with his uncertainty. Planned completion: the practical reason to leave home. Do not solve surrender by declaring it loss of agency.

Envelope: **4,100–5,900 words**. Possible episode ending: the family exchange and its emotional aftermath, once he has a legible immediate next step. A wanted-image reveal alone would be a weaker ending if it bypassed that consequence. No manuscript boundary is moved to achieve this.

## Sequence 2 — The Weight of a Decision

Existing cards: **5 Somewhere to hide → 6 Claire and Roxana save Rain**. Keep both complete cards; the encounter remains unfinished.

1. **Dramatic question:** can Rain respond to an injured person's need when he is frightened, exposed, and unprepared?
2. **Immediate desire:** find shelter; then understand what he has encountered and respond to Roxana. The author requests a voluntary attempt to assist her. Its exact action and practical effect remain to be written.
3. **External conflict:** her injury and insistence that he leave, his limited ability, and the arriving armed unit. The flash draws his attention in the draft; its cause is not established.
4. **Emotional progression:** isolation and vigilance → attention to another person → concern and hesitation → an attempted response meeting limits → dependence on Claire's intervention. Inadequacy is a specific limitation, not a verdict on his worth.
5. **Climax:** the requested help attempt meets a real limit; Claire's existing bullet-interception introduction changes the immediate outcome. No invented tactical success, power, or cost duration is assigned to Rain.
6. **Payoff/consequence:** immediate survival becomes possible through others. They have commitments beyond him; his situation now includes people whose choices he cannot direct. Escape should have a legible method and cost, not just impressive competence.
7. **Carry-forward question:** who are these people, why will they help him, and what does accepting their help involve? Those questions have an emotional landing after danger, without a compulsory villain reveal.
8. **Setups:** Roxana's faltering stance lets her later competence change the meaning of apparent weakness; Claire's intervention establishes skill before biography. Keep their motives independent. Establish what the soldiers know and why force escalates before treating the confrontation as inevitable. Do not reveal Vesper's operation to manufacture suspense.

Scene intentions and provisional envelopes:
- **5 Somewhere to hide — 600–1,000:** shelter-seeking changes his relationship to the familiar city; the brief flash redirects his attention. Preserve the existing observations rather than adding another abstract debate about his whole life.
- **6 Claire and Roxana save Rain — 2,200–3,200:** preserve the encounter, finish the choice to assist, encounter his limit, and make Claire's intervention and the exit intelligible. The EVENTS list is a working sketch, not completed prose or locked mechanics.

Envelope: **2,800–4,200 words**. Possible episode ending: the danger has genuinely subsided and Rain can register that he needed—and received—help. Avoid introducing a fresh attack only to produce a cliffhanger.

## Sequence 3 — Choosing to Continue

Existing planned cards: **7 The hideout → 8 The trail**. Existing placeholders remain; the guide is not substitute manuscript prose.

1. **Dramatic question:** once he has shelter and time to answer, does Rain choose a next step himself?
2. **Immediate desire:** safety, understanding, connection, and a reason to move. His personal reason to accompany the others is open. Their established operational objective is to follow the disappearance trail toward the asylum.
3. **External conflict:** what the group knows, needs, can offer, and risks by including him; the practical alternatives available to Rain. The family situation and fugitive status persist without being settled in a speech.
4. **Emotional progression:** relief → interest and comparison with their competence → accepted help and provisional connection → awareness of the cost of going → a chosen response with doubt intact.
5. **Climax:** he chooses to accompany them when staying is a credible option. He does not need to announce a permanent rejection of his family or previous life.
6. **Payoff/revelation:** shelter is real help but does not choose his future for him. A concrete trail gives direction; it does not certify the theft or promise exoneration. The pleasant asylum threshold hands painful wanting to Arc 2.
7. **Carry-forward question:** why continue wanting when it hurts—and what might apparently effective relief offer him? Preserve the asylum's initial plausibility rather than framing it as obviously sinister.
8. **Setups:** Roxana's quick welcome and Claire solving a small problem are existing planned seeds, not proof that either can live his life for him. Give their stated reasons room to be true and incomplete. The clue connecting disappearances to the asylum must be information readers receive, not Claire's unsupported assertion.

Scene intentions and provisional envelopes:
- **7 The hideout — 900–1,400:** let an ordinary exchange establish a relationship, practical shelter, and distinct motives; leave complete biographies and diagnoses for later. No scene scenario is prescribed here.
- **8 The trail — 1,200–1,800:** establish the actual lead, the available alternatives, Rain's particular motive, and the decision to go. An opportunity to consider family/contact belongs here only if it affects the decision; no automatic reconciliation.

Envelope: **2,100–3,200 words**. Possible episode ending: his chosen continuation and the welcoming destination. This sequence is a useful final movement, but has not yet earned a whole anime episode. If it stays brief, let it close the previous episode instead of padding it.

## Mystery and interaction discipline

Use the six construction principles supplied in the request: a current question, partial answers, changed significance, motivated inquiry, apparent resolution opening deeper conflict, and emotional endings. The official [Link Click episode page](https://www.bilibili.tv/en/play/1006275?s_locale=en_US) was consulted, but did not provide an inspectable full episode/transcript. This is application of the author's requested principles, not a claimed shot-by-shot audit of Episode 1.

Question ladder: **What draws him off-route? → what did he choose to take? → what does the public claim that choice means? → can he respond to the injured woman? → what kind of help has he received? → why go with them?** Each sequence answers a local question while leaving a more consequential one. Do not make every sensory detail a secret clue or invent a conspiracy to connect them.

Optional VN opportunities, not new rules: inspect the notebook, environment, and goggles; vary attention or timing around familiar routine and shelter; explore optional conversation texture at the hideout. Necessary causal information must still appear on one attentive playthrough. Keep the author's theft/help/continuation actions legible in the manuscript; do not replace them with a correct-answer menu. The Go home pitfall's placement and reconvergence remain unresolved in `Choice Points`; this pass does not assign them. Anime boundaries are adaptation candidates only; no fixed runtime or new POV cut is imposed.

2. **Accusation and accountability:** what establishes identification and the disproportionate disappearance allegation? What would cooperation actually require? Keep the two-object draft conflict visible until the author revises it.

5. **Asylum choice:** what evidence establishes the destination and what does Rain personally want enough to accompany them? Answers, concern for the others, and connection are different candidate motives, not a required combination.
