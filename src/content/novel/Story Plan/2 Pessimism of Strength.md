## Pessimism of Strength

This is the story's authorial stance, not dialogue for a character to deliver.

Reality does not guarantee justice, meaning, success, permanence, or happiness. Its beauty does not cancel its horror, and its horror does not cancel its beauty.

A person enters that reality embodied, conditioned, dependent, and unfinished. Nobody creates themselves from nothing. Agency is the local capacity to respond within conditions one did not fully choose, and that capacity changes across people and moments.

Life is becoming; agency is the ability to participate in it.

Strength is not domination, productivity, invulnerability, or the ability to tolerate unlimited suffering. It is the capacity to encounter reality, be changed by it, and still make a deliberate response. That response may be action, refusal, rest, grief, help accepted, a changed direction, or recognition that something cannot be repaired.

Commitment does not guarantee a good result. It allows a choice to acquire consequences. Past choices can become modest evidence for self-trust: *I have acted before; I may act again.* They become a cage when they are required to settle the self permanently.

Care may guide, protect, confront, restrain, or relieve pain. Its limit is another person's continuing ability to participate. Relief that restores that ability is medicine. Relief that removes the person who could later accept, regret, revise, or refuse it is subtraction.

The story's claim is narrow:

> See reality without demanding a promise from it, then participate anyway.

No character embodies the whole answer. Rain's slowness harms people. Claire's models are necessary. Roxana's care is real. The Pursuer's endurance is formidable. Vesper correctly sees suffering others tolerate. Shiori's making is also a way not to ask. Their value is what gives each distortion force.

The ending does not prove Rain is fixed. It shows him choosing one next step without public vindication, certainty, or felt proof that his past belongs to him.
