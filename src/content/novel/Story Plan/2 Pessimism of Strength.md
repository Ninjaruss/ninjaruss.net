## Pessimism of Strength

The story's central pressure is whether a limited person can participate in reality without treating strength, success, or accumulated experience as proof that they are complete. This is an authorial stance, not dialogue for a character to deliver.

### Reality and limitation

Reality does not guarantee justice, meaning, success, permanence, or happiness. Its beauty does not cancel its horror, and its horror does not cancel its beauty.

A person enters that reality embodied, conditioned, dependent, and unfinished. Nobody creates themselves from nothing. Agency is the local capacity to respond within conditions one did not fully choose, and that capacity changes across people and moments.

Limitation does not erase agency, and agency does not erase limitation.

### Strength

Strength is not domination, productivity, invulnerability, or the ability to tolerate unlimited suffering. It is the capacity to encounter reality, be changed by it, and still make a deliberate response. That response may be action, refusal, rest, grief, help accepted, a changed direction, or recognition that something cannot be repaired.

Capacity is not proof of worth, correctness, or future success. Greater capacity can widen responsibility without turning a person into a finished answer.

### Participation and proof

Agency is the ability to participate in becoming. Commitment does not guarantee a good result; it allows a choice to acquire consequences. Past choices can support modest self-trust—*I have acted before; I may act again*—but become a cage when required to settle the self permanently.

The temptation is to replace present participation with proof: unrealized potential, a correct model, endurance, successful treatment, a preserved record, or a completed story. None can make the next response in advance.

### Care and subtraction

Care may guide, protect, confront, restrain, or relieve pain. Its limit is another person's continuing ability to participate. Relief that restores that ability is medicine. Relief that removes the person who could later accept, regret, revise, or refuse it is subtraction.

No character embodies the whole answer. Their strengths remain valuable; the story tests what happens when any one strength is asked to decide the person completely.
