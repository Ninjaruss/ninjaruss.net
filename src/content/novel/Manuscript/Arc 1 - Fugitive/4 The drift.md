The ceiling here had a water stain shaped like nothing in particular.

I had looked at it long enough to be sure of that. In the morning it was grey, and by the afternoon it went a kind of brown, and I had started to look forward to the hour it changed.

No alarm. That was the first thing I had noticed about this place, and I kept noticing it every morning, as though it were news.

7 AM.

I don't have work today.

Outside, the rain kept on. It hit the window at the same angle it hit the window at home, and with my eyes shut I could not have told anyone which room I was in.

…

I peeled the blanket away.

The pan was already on the burner. I had set it out the night before so the morning would go smoother, and I stood there for a moment with my hand on the handle, trying to remember deciding to do that.

Two eggs. Half a packet of something I had stopped looking at closely. I ate at a table lower than mine, with one leg shimmed by a folded receipt I had folded tighter on the second day, and the whole of it took eleven minutes. I knew it took eleven minutes because it had taken eleven minutes yesterday.

I left a job to stop doing this.

The notebook sat on the table where I had put it the first night, squared to the edge, where I would have to see it.

I spun the pen.

What should I do today?

The question arrived the way it always did. The answer was eight inches from my hand, and I looked past it the way you look past someone you owe money to.

The window latch was loose. That was real — it rattled all night and I had been listening to it rattle for three nights, and a person who could not fix a latch had no business claiming he was serious about anything.

I opened the notebook.

Window latch.

The tap in the bathroom ran warm before it ran cold, which was backwards, and I wrote that down too. The light in the hall took four seconds to decide. The bin needed something done about it. I got most of the way down the page before I noticed what my handwriting looked like, lined up like that, one line each, nothing under any of them.

…

I closed it.

Not yet. I should get the room sorted first. It's hard to think in a room like this.

The goggles were in the drawer under the bed, where they had been since the first night, and I had not opened the drawer. I knew exactly how far it was from where I was sitting. I had known that all morning, the way you know a sound has stopped.

I took the latch apart instead. One screw was stripped. I sat on the floor with the parts in my lap and the rain coming down outside and the phone face-down on the table with things on it I had not opened, and I thought: this is fine. This is a day where something gets done.

That was when the light in the hall changed.

It did not take four seconds.

The figure stood past the doorway, faded gold at the edges, one hand open and turned up toward the ceiling the way you hold a hand out to show it is empty. It did not point at the drawer. It did not point at the notebook. It was only there, with its palm up, asking for nothing I could have named.

I stayed on the floor.

My hands kept working. I set the screw in and turned it and it caught nothing and I turned it again.

…

I have a latch to fix.

…

It waited.

I have a latch to fix, and I have a phone full of people who want to know what I've done to my life, and I have eleven minutes of eggs and a page of things that need doing, and if I stand up right now I don't get any of that back.

The pen was still in my other hand. I had not noticed picking it up.
